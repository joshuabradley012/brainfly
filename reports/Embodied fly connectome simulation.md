# Closing the Loop From Connectome to Fly

Two complete fly central nervous systems, brain and nerve cord in one animal, are now mapped synapse by synapse: the male MaleCNS v1.0 (**166,700 neurons, 11,710 types**) and the female BANC. The biology around them is known well enough to trace the whole loop. Roughly 900 eye columns and thousands of other sensory neurons transduce the world outside the connectome. A brain sends its commands down about **1,300 descending neurons**. A nerve cord, where per-leg rhythm generators and local sensory feedback pattern movement, drives about **700 motor neurons**, whose muscles have measured mechanics for barely one motor pool. What the map leaves out is most of what makes it compute: which neurons spike and which are graded, receptor-dependent synapse signs, electrical synapses, neuromodulators, and muscle and joint parameters. Every model validated against real flies covers one slice. Shiu's whole-brain spiking model got **91% of 164 predictions** right, almost all of them in feeding and grooming circuits. The flyvis graded optic lobe was checked against **26 studies**. Pugliese's rate-model nerve cord predicted that the descending neuron DNb08 drives rhythmic leg movement, which optogenetics then confirmed. Each succeeds by matching unit type to brain region and fitting a few parameters per cell type. Nobody, Eon Systems' March 2026 "embodied fly" included, has closed a validated loop from connectome through body. The fly.ai model has three headline failures: light dies at the lamina, descending commands never reach motor neurons, and scrambled wiring signals as well as real wiring. Each follows predictably from choices no validated model combines: identical spiking units where neurons are graded, per-neuron input normalisation that dilutes commands by fan-in, a 100 ms time constant stepped at 20 ms, and gross metrics that a degree-preserving scramble passes by construction. The way forward is a ladder. Reproduce Shiu's validated recipe on MaleCNS first. Then add a graded optic lobe fed by synthesised photoreceptor input, a size-scaled nerve cord, curated gap junctions, a FlyGym 2.x body with muscles on the one leg where they exist, and finally modulatory state and plasticity. Each rung has to pass a pre-registered real-fly benchmark and a null-wiring control before the next one starts.

## A complete connectome still leaves the fly's dynamics underdetermined

The project's substrate is exceptional. The peer-reviewed male CNS reports **166,700 neurons and 11,710 types** ([Berg et al., Cell 2026](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00942-6)), with 46 million presynapses, synapse precision and recall of 0.82/0.81, and a type match to FlyWire, hemibrain or MANC for 97.5% of neurons ([Berg et al. preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/)). It has been public under CC-BY 4.0 through neuPrint since 8 June 2026 ([male-cns portal](https://male-cns.janelia.org/)). Sex differences sit mainly in higher brain centres: 8,069 types are isomorphic between sexes and only 138 dimorphic, 289 male-specific and 71 female-specific, while the sensory and motor periphery is "largely isomorphic" ([Berg et al. preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/)). That matters because nearly all validation data come from females. Four sister datasets supply cross-checks. **FlyWire** has 139,255 neurons in a female brain with no nerve cord, under a non-commercial licence ([Dorkenwald et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07558-y)). **BANC** has about 160,000 neurons in a female brain plus nerve cord ([Bates et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13518251/)). **MANC** has over 23,000 neurons in a male nerve cord ([Takemura et al. 2024](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/97769)). **FANC**, a female nerve cord, maps leg and wing motor neurons to their muscles ([Azevedo et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11348827/)).

The map is also incomplete in ways a simulation feels directly. Only **42% of postsynaptic sites** are attached to proofread neurons, and **40.1% of connections** have both partners proofread ([Berg et al. preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/)). Connection weights vary "surprisingly" within and between animals. Connections above 10 synapses, or above 1% of a target's input, are the ones that are highly conserved ([Schlegel et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07686-5)).

More fundamentally, electron microscopy leaves out whole categories of biology. Gap junctions are below its resolution in every published fly connectome ([Lesser et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11356479/)). Transmitter identity is predicted by a classifier that is **87% accurate per synapse and 94% per neuron**, but it cannot see roughly 53 neuropeptides ([Eckstein et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11106717/)). MaleCNS's recommended `consensusNt` field sets every octopamine and serotonin call to "unclear" ([Berg et al. preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/)). Receptor types, intrinsic excitability, and muscle and joint mechanics are absent altogether.

Theory and history both warn that this gap is not cosmetic. In a teacher-student study, "a connectome is often insufficient to constrain the dynamics of networks that perform a specific task." Recordings from a small subset of neurons, though, remove most of the degeneracy and predict the unrecorded cells ([Beiran & Litwin-Kumar 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-02080-4)). The worm is the cautionary case. OpenWorm launched in 2011 with a complete connectome ([Sarma et al. 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6158220/)). Yet measured signal propagation across 23,433 neuron pairs still "differs from model predictions that are based on anatomy," partly because of extrasynaptic signalling ([Randi et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06683-4)). The sharpest 2026 warning came from the Brunton and Tuthill labs. A *C. elegans* connectome with a reinforcement-learned decoder produced "highly realistic fly walking" in a fly body, which led the authors to conclude that "behavioral fidelity is achievable without biological fidelity" ([digital sphinx, bioRxiv 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.713233v1)).

The models that have passed real-fly tests share three traits. They use graded or rate units wherever the biology is graded. They share parameters across cells of a type: flyvis uses 734 free parameters for 45,669 neurons ([Lappalainen et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11525180/)), and ring-attractor fits need only three effective scale factors ([Biswas et al. preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.01.621596v2.full)). And they are fitted to a task or a small amount of data, then scored on held-out physiology. The rest of this report walks through the fly layer by layer with that template in mind.

## Every sense is transduced outside the connectome

Every EM connectome contains only the central axon terminals of sensory neurons. Somata, dendrites and transduction sites sit in the periphery, so a simulated fly must model each sense outside the connectome and treat the connectome's sensory neurons as spike generators driven by those models. MaleCNS is the best-covered substrate for this: it includes the nerve cord, so leg, wing and haltere afferents are present, which they are not in FlyWire. But some photoreceptors, Johnston's organ neurons and olfactory receptor neurons were lost to edge artefacts and segmentation problems ([Berg et al. preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/)). BANC lacks the laminae entirely, about 9,390 R1–6 and Lai neurons ([Bates et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13518251/)).

### Vision is graded and sign-inverting from the first synapse

The female eye has about 750 ommatidia. They cover a near-hemisphere with under 20° of binocular overlap and a posterior blind spot of about 50° ([Zhao et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12488493/)). The male right optic lobe has **892 medulla columns** ([Nern et al. preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11042306/)). Neighbouring ommatidia sit about 4.5° apart, and each dark-adapted R1–6 photoreceptor accepts light from an 8.2° cone, with 6.2 ms latency and reliable encoding up to about 43 Hz at 19 °C ([Gonzalez-Bellido et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3054003/)). R1–6 cells from six neighbouring ommatidia view the same point, and this neural superposition improves signal-to-noise by up to √6 ([Juusola et al. 2017](https://elifesciences.org/articles/26117)). Photoreceptors never spike. Each is a graded integrator of about 30,000 stochastic microvilli ([Song et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3420010/)), whose impulse response speeds from 40 ms to 20 ms time-to-peak as light brightens ([Juusola & Hardie 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2232468/)), with a temperature Q10 of about 2.5 ([Juusola & Hardie 2001b](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2232470/)).

The decisive fact for simulation is the first synapse. Photoreceptors release **histamine onto chloride channels**, so lamina monopolar cells *hyperpolarise* to light, and flies lacking the receptor are behaviourally blind ([Pantazis et al. 2008](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/28/29/7250)). These cells rest at −40 to −70 mV and swing by up to 45 mV ([Zheng et al. 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2151524/)). The sign flips back one synapse later. Glutamatergic L1 inverts through GluClα channels onto ON cells ([Molina-Obando et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.49373)). The ON inputs (Mi1, Tm3) and OFF inputs (Tm1, Tm2) act as band-pass filters whose measured responses peak 43–71 ms after the stimulus ([Behnia et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4243710/)). Two consequences follow. First, a spiking unit that is silent at rest cannot carry the light signal, because light arrives as inhibition. Second, responses at visual projection neurons should lag the stimulus by tens of milliseconds. A chain of 1.8 ms spiking synapses reaches them far too fast unless graded stages or explicit filters are added.

### Chemical senses have quantitative tuning tables and adaptive dynamics

Olfaction is the best-parameterised sense. DoOR 2.0 merges 7,381 measurements into a normalised table of **693 odorants by 78 receptor units**, each mapped to a glomerulus. It has no concentration or time axis, though ([Münch & Galizia 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4766438/)). Receptor neurons first transduce odour through ligand–receptor kinetics with adaptive feedback. They then encode it through a stereotyped, differentiating spike filter that follows odour fluctuations up to about 6 Hz ([Nagel & Wilson 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3030680/)), with Weber-like gain control over roughly 300 ms ([Gorur-Shandilya et al. 2017](https://elifesciences.org/articles/27670)). Projection neurons transform receptor input through a saturating ratio with exponent 1.5 plus divisive lateral gain control ([Olsen et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2866644/)). Each receptor-neuron spike produces a **6.2 mV unitary EPSP** in its projection neuron ([Kazama & Wilson 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2429849/)). Even without odour, the brain receives about **20,000 receptor-neuron spikes per second** from roughly 1,200 neurons per antenna ([Wilson 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3933953/)). That spontaneous barrage has to be absorbed stably. NeuroMechFly v2 already supplies a 2-D fluid plume and antennal odour sensors ([NeuroMechFly v2 postprint](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf)), and real walking-fly plume statistics give a target to match ([Demir et al. 2020](https://elifesciences.org/articles/57524)).

Taste runs through five receptor classes: sweet, bitter, water, high salt and low salt ([Jaeger et al. 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/37167)). Each labellar palp has 31 taste hairs. The strongest bitter response recorded at a hair tip is only about 61 spikes/s ([Weiss et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3033050/)), whereas Shiu's model drove taste neurons at 50–200 Hz ([Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/)). The rate scale is therefore a free choice, and it has to be calibrated. The complete male gustatory system, including leg, wing and pharyngeal taste neurons and all feeding motor neurons, is now mapped in the same male specimen ([Tastekin et al., Cell 2026](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00943-8)).

### Mechanosensors report the body's own movement, and some of that report is gated

The antenna's Johnston's organ has about 500 neurons. About 200 of them (classes A and B) detect sound-driven vibrations as small as 50 nm, and about 250 (classes C and E) report sustained deflection from gravity and wind ([Albert & Göpfert 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4582067/)). Active amplification tunes the receiver to about **184 Hz**; without it, the best frequency is about 800 Hz ([Kavlie et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4282873/)). That matches courtship song, whose pulses peak near 220 Hz and whose sine song runs at 120–180 Hz ([Clemens et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6830513/)), with pulses about 35 ms apart ([Stern 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4071150/)). Bristle neurons fire to deflections under 10 µm, and a single bristle spike reliably evokes EPSPs in every connected central cell class ([Tuthill & Wilson 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4879191/)). The head alone has over 1,000 bristles, and 705 of their neurons have been mapped somatotopically onto grooming pathways ([Eichler et al. 2024](https://elifesciences.org/articles/87602)).

Leg proprioception is split by function. In the femoral chordotonal organ, claw neurons encode tibia position tonically, hook neurons encode directional movement, and club neurons encode movement plus 100–2,000 Hz vibration ([Mamiya et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6481666/)). The split is set mechanically by separate tendons and a structure called the arculum ([Mamiya et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10644877/)). Crucially, **hook axons are presynaptically inhibited during self-generated walking and grooming** by GABAergic 9A interneurons driven by descending pathways. The same study published encoding models that map femur–tibia angle and velocity directly onto each class's activity ([Dallmann et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13070307/)). Other load and joint sensors are mapped too. Hair plates, 214 neurons in 42 plates across the six legs, act as joint-limit detectors that trigger postural reflexes ([Pratt et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13009157/)). Campaniform sensilla sense cuticular strain, and a finite-element model predicts their loading during stepping ([Dinges et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12056673/)). The halteres carry about 400 afferents across both sides. In FANC, 4,479 neurons receive 69,199 synapses from left-side haltere afferents alone, and those afferents reach wing-steering motor neurons almost directly ([Dhawan et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12872070/)).

Temperature and humidity sensing is small but precise. Three warming and three cooling neurons on each arista respond to 0.5 °C changes ([Gallio et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3336488/)), and dry and humid air reach dedicated glomeruli ([Marin et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7443704/)). Internal-state sensors, such as brain Gr43a neurons that detect fructose in the hemolymph, are slow and could enter a simulation as gradually varying biases ([Miyamoto et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3509419/)).

## Fly neurons span a hundredfold range of input resistance, and many never spike

A connectome simulation first has to decide what kind of unit each neuron is. For the fly, the direct evidence comes from a patchwork of single-cell-type recordings. No database of spiking status or intrinsic properties exists ([male-cns portal](https://male-cns.janelia.org/)), and the reference whole-brain model explicitly "does not account for gap junctions, non-spiking neurons, internal state or long-range neuropeptides" ([Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/)).

### Graded signalling dominates the eye and parts of the nerve cord

Graded or nonspiking signalling has been recorded throughout the early visual system. It covers photoreceptors and lamina monopolar cells ([Zheng et al. 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2151524/)) and the medulla ON and OFF inputs Mi1, Tm3, Tm1 and Tm2 ([Behnia et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4243710/)). It also covers T4, whose recordings show only occasional 1–2 mV transients that "could not be verified as spikes" ([Gruntman et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-017-0046-4)), and the wide-field amacrine CT1, whose terminals act as independent units ([Meier & Borst 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.03.070)). Beyond the eye, the mushroom body's inhibitory APL neuron is nonspiking ([Amin et al. 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7541083/)), and so are "patchy" antennal-lobe local neurons ([Schenk & Gaudry 2023](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0109-22.2022)). Even descending neurons vary: DNOVS1 is nonspiking, while DNOVS2 and DNHS1 fire small spikes on top of graded potentials ([Suver et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2277-16.2016)). In the nerve cord, the leg proprioceptive interneurons 13Bα and 10Bα showed no identifiable action potentials ([Agrawal et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.60299)).

Spiking is documented in olfactory receptor and projection neurons, Kenyon cells, mushroom-body output neurons, central-complex compass neurons, the giant fiber and the steering descending neurons, and motor neurons (sources in the table below). Expression of the fly's only sodium-channel gene, *para*, cannot settle the question. It marks 94% of adult neurons ([Ravenscroft et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0142-20.2020)), and the protein sits on the axons of T4 and T5, which are graded ([Fendl et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.62953)).

A defensible first-pass prior treats all photoreceptors, lamina cells, medulla columnar cells, T4/T5 and amacrine-like cells as graded, along with the specific recorded nerve-cord interneurons. It defaults everything else to spiking. The optic-lobe part of that flag is large: one optic lobe alone holds about 53,000 neurons ([Nern et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08746-0)).

### Intrinsic properties vary by cell type and track neuron size

Input resistance varies about **a hundredfold across recorded types**, roughly in inverse proportion to neuron size. Membrane time constants derived from fitted cable parameters run from about 2 ms in giant descending neurons to about 30 ms in projection neurons. That is far shorter than fly.ai's 100 ms.

Fly neurons are unipolar. The soma hangs off a thin neurite outside the signalling path, spikes start in the proximal axon, and somatic voltage is "severely attenuated in the axon." The same study shows that whole-cell seals bias measured resting potentials several millivolts too depolarised ([Gouwens & Wilson 2009](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0764-09.2009)). Somatic values should therefore not be copied literally into point models. Some visual interneurons are "multi-output devices" that are neither point neurons nor independent compartments ([Seung 2025 preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.02.636137)). The measured heterogeneity has a practical upshot: excitability can be estimated from EM morphology, because larger cells have lower input resistance, without recording every type.

### Synapse counts predict strength only after normalising by the target's size

Fast synaptic events are brief. Nicotinic currents rise in 0.6–1.5 ms and decay with τ ≈ 2–7 ms ([Lee & O'Dowd 1999](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.19-13-05311.1999); [Gu & O'Dowd 2006](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4109-05.2006)), and GABA-A currents decay in about 5 ms ([Lee, Su & O'Dowd 2003](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-11-04625.2003)). Slower components also shape behaviour. GABA-B inhibition lasts "∼100 ms to several seconds" ([Wilson & Laurent 2005](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2070-05.2005)). Interglomerular inhibition acts largely *presynaptically*, on receptor-neuron terminals, as gain control ([Olsen & Wilson 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06864)). The receptor-neuron-to-projection-neuron synapse depresses: each spike scales conductance by 0.78, and recovery takes 893 ms ([Nagel, Hong & Wilson 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3895)).

Count-versus-strength has been tested directly in only a few places. In lateral-horn neurons, **synapse density** (count divided by postsynaptic surface area) predicted unitary EPSP amplitude with r² = 0.77 for dendritic inputs but badly underpredicted axonal ones ([Liu et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.11.056)). In the antennal lobe, synapse number rises with projection-neuron dendrite size and "precisely compensates" for dendritic filtering ([Tobin et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.24838)). Per-synapse efficacy at the spike zone still differs about threefold between two looming-responsive descending neurons ([Moreno-Sanchez et al. preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.24.591016)). Some anatomical connections are functionally silent and can reorganise within minutes ([Fisher et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1772-4)). The evidence favours weighting by synapse count divided by target size, or by an input-resistance proxy. Weighting by a neuron's total input count, as fly.ai does, is less well supported.

### Transmitter identity is a weak proxy for synapse sign

Transmitter predictions are good overall: **87% per synapse, 94% per neuron and 91% per cell type** ([Eckstein et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.03.016)). They fail in the places that matter most for sign. GABA and glutamate are the most confused pair, and serotonin calls are right only about a third of the time ([Eckstein et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.03.016)). In-situ labelling (FISH) confirmed only 3 of 9 GABA predictions for MANC descending neurons ([Cheong et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13384506/)). One live conflict falls inside the walking rhythm generator discussed below. INXXX466 is the network's E2 neuron. It is predicted cholinergic in MANC but belongs to a glutamatergic hemilineage in FANC ([Pugliese et al. preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13142387/)).

Receptors can override the transmitter's usual sign. Glutamate usually inhibits through the chloride channel GluClα; antennal-lobe recordings "never observed a depolarizing response" ([Liu & Wilson 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1220560110)). In T4 the same inhibition is divisive shunting rather than subtraction ([Groschner et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04428-3)). But Dm8 excites Tm5c through a kainate receptor ([Karuppudurai et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.12.010)), and about 80% of central neurons express both GluClα and an excitatory glutamate receptor ([Eckstein et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.03.016)). Acetylcholine inhibits Kenyon cells through muscarinic receptors ([Bielopolski et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.48264)). The stakes are measurable: making glutamate excitatory in Shiu's model raised its false-positive rate from **1% to 16%** ([Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/)). No whole-CNS receptor-informed sign map exists yet. Transcriptomic atlases for the optic lobe ([Davis et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.50901)), nerve cord ([Allen et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.54074)) and central brain ([Allen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2025.101103)) could be used to build one.

### Electrical synapses, neuromodulators and plasticity are invisible but load-bearing

The fly has eight innexins. ShakB is the most widely expressed neuronal one ([Ammer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2022.03.040)). Documented electrical synapses sit at critical nodes. The giant fiber's outputs to the jump-muscle motor neuron (TTMn) and to the PSI interneuron that relays to the flight power muscles are mixed, rectifying electrical-chemical synapses ([Phelan et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2008.10.067)). Without ShakB, giant-fiber-to-jump-muscle latency rises from 0.7–1.2 ms to about 1.5 ms, and the flight-muscle branch fails ([Augustin et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.3791/2412)). Excitatory local neurons excite antennal-lobe projection neurons *only* electrically ([Yaksi & Wilson 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.08.041)). Weakly coupled flight motor neurons fire in ordered splay sequences rather than together ([Hürkey et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06099-0)). No connectome annotates any of these, so they must be added as a curated edge layer.

Neuromodulation acts through GPCRs over hundreds of milliseconds to hours, mostly as gain and kinetic changes. Several effects have measured sizes. Flight **doubles** the visual responses of VS neurons through octopamine ([Maimon et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2492); [Suver et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2012.10.034)). Octopamine speeds up the inputs to T4/T5, shifting their temporal tuning toward faster motion ([Arenz et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2017.01.051)). Hunger raises the gain of Or42b receptor neurons through sNPF signalling ([Root et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2011.02.008)).

Shiu's model missed the strong phenotype of Usnea neurons. Knocking down a neuropeptide-processing enzyme in them mimicked silencing, and the authors warn that "circuits with extensive neuromodulation or extrasynaptic signalling will be poorly modelled" ([Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/)). Treating dopamine, octopamine and serotonin synapses as fast excitatory edges, as fly.ai's sign rule does, is the wrong abstraction. They belong in slow modulatory state variables.

Plasticity is best characterised in the mushroom body. Pairing a 1 s odour with dopamine-neuron activation cut the output neuron's odour response by **80% in spikes and 90% in synaptic charge for at least 40 minutes** ([Hige et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.11.003)). The rule depends on timing. Forward pairing depresses the synapse and backward pairing potentiates it, through different dopamine receptors ([Handler et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.040)). Compartments differ in learning rate and memory lifetime ([Aso & Rubin 2016](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.16135)). The mapping from visual ring neurons onto compass neurons also remaps within minutes ([Fisher et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1772-4)).

Taken together, the biophysics argues for a **hybrid per-type model**. Threshold-linear graded units would cover the optic lobe's columnar and amacrine cells, APL and the recorded nonspiking interneurons. Projection neurons would be spiking units at sub-millisecond steps, or saturating rate units. Slow synaptic states, curated gap junctions, modulatory state and plasticity sit on top.

An energy budget also caps what "resting" can mean. One estimate from oxygen consumption limits mean firing across the brain to about **4 Hz** ([State of Brain Emulation Report 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15745)).

## Validated brain models match the unit to the region and fit a few parameters per type

### Shiu's whole-brain model works for short spiking chains and fails at disinhibition

The reference whole-brain model is Shiu et al.'s leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) network of **127,400 FlyWire neurons**. All neurons share one set of parameters: rest −52 mV, threshold −45 mV, τ 20 ms, refractory period 2.2 ms, synaptic decay 5 ms and delay 1.8 ms. The single free parameter, **W_syn = 0.275 mV per synapse**, multiplies raw synapse counts, with no normalisation, and baseline activity is zero ([Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/)). The reference reimplementations run it at 0.1 ms steps ([eonsystemspbc/fly-brain](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain)). W_syn was calibrated on one pathway: 100 Hz sugar-receptor drive should produce about 80% of maximal firing in MN9, the motor neuron for proboscis extension.

Of 164 testable predictions, 91% held. The model also found real novelties. Ir94e taste neurons inhibit feeding. JO-CE mechanosensors, but not JO-F, activate the grooming interneuron aBN1, even though JO-F synapses on it directly. Accuracy stayed at 85–88% when W_syn varied by ±30%. With the real wiring, sugar input activated MN9 in every run; with the weights shuffled, it did so in 1 of 100 ([Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/)). The same model helped identify two halting mechanisms: GABAergic "walk-OFF" neurons in the brain and cholinergic "brake" neurons in the nerve cord ([Sapkal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07854-7)).

The limits are just as instructive. Almost every validated prediction lies in short feedforward spiking chains from taste or mechanosensory neurons, none visual. The authors note that with zero basal firing, activating inhibitory neurons "cannot alter the firing of downstream neurons," so disinhibition is invisible to the model ([Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/)). No independent lab has re-tested the 91% figure against biology. Existing replications check only that simulators agree numerically ([eonsystemspbc/fly-brain](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain)).

### flyvis shows how a task and 734 shared parameters recover visual physiology

The best-validated circuit model is flyvis ([Lappalainen et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11525180/)). It has **45,669 neurons in 64 cell types**, arranged on a hexagonal lattice 31 columns across and modelled as passive, non-spiking units. Synapse counts and signs are fixed from EM and transmitter profiling. Only 734 parameters are free: 65 resting potentials, 65 time constants and 604 type-to-type synapse scale factors. They are trained end to end on optic-flow estimation.

An ensemble of 50 models was compared with 26 published studies. The model predicted the ON/OFF contrast preference of at least 30 of 32 characterised cell types and produced T4 and T5 direction selectivity from untuned inputs. Better task performance predicted more realistic direction selectivity (r = 0.60). Connectomes with random parameters kept contrast preference but lost direction selectivity. Degeneracy was explicit: T4c solutions fell into three clusters of near-equal task error, two direction-selective and one not. The code and pretrained ensembles are public ([TuragaLab/flyvis](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flyvis)).

The lesson for MaleCNS: the optic lobe needs graded units with fitted per-type parameters. One global LIF gain is not enough.

### Other circuits confirm the recipe: type-level scale factors plus a discriminating recording

**Head direction.** A threshold-linear EPG–Delta7 model needs only three effective scale factors. With them, all four connectomes (MaleCNS, hemibrain, FlyWire, BANC) admit three exact ring-attractor solutions. The solutions reproduce EPG activity equally well but predict 8, 6 or 4 active Delta7 cells, so one Delta7 recording would discriminate among them ([Biswas et al. preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.01.621596v2.full)). Ring attractors also emerged across wide parameter ranges in earlier spiking and non-spiking models ([Kakaria & de Bivort 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5306390/)).

**Escape.** Looming-sensitive LPLC2 neurons drive the giant fiber through dorsoventral synaptic gradients ([Dombrovski et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05562-8)). The timing of the giant fiber's spike relative to parallel pathways selects a fast or a controlled take-off ([von Reyn et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3741)).

**Courtship vision.** Knockout training on 459 courting pairs showed that combinations of lobula columnar types drive courtship, a population code rather than one type per behaviour ([Cowley et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07451-8)). That warns against hand-assigning one behaviour per visual type (for example, LC10a as "chase").

**Mushroom body and antennal lobe.** I found no quantitatively validated connectome-dynamics model of either on FlyWire or MaleCNS. The closest work is an APL model with spatially localised inhibition ([Amin et al. 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7541083/)) and critiques arguing that the wiring alone is insufficient ([Lazar & Zhou preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19290)).

### Whole-brain dynamics need fitting, and wiring specificity shows up only in routing

An untrained FlyWire LIF "failed to reproduce any experimentally observed spontaneous fluctuations." After its weights were trained (signs fixed) against whole-brain resting-state imaging across 68 neuropils, it matched functional connectivity on held-out data. Training used about 8.9 GB of GPU memory with an online learning rule ([Wang et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12913608/)). Region-level structure predicts resting functional connectivity, though the mushroom body leans more on indirect paths ([Turner, Mann & Clandinin 2021](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(21)00343-2)).

BANC's whole-CNS influence analysis adds a systems-level fact: effector neurons get their strongest influence from sensors in the same body part, so control is "distributed, parallel and embodied" ([Bates et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13518251/)). A larval-connectome study sharpens the null-model question. Gross gain and dimensionality depend almost entirely on degree and weight statistics, within a few percent of rewired ensembles. The routing of input and the mushroom-body modes, by contrast, are "written into its exact wiring" ([Therianos preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17745)).

Forward simulation is not the bottleneck. An Apple-Silicon port runs MaleCNS in 1.17 s per biological second on a single M4 Pro machine ([drosophila-brain-mlx, unreviewed](https://github.com/Kisame76/drosophila-brain-mlx)). Loihi 2 beats a CPU simulator by 3–350× but runs in real time only when activity is sparse ([Wang et al. preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16792)). The real cost is gradient-based fitting.

## The nerve cord, not the brain, turns descending commands into rhythm

### About 1,300 descending neurons act more like a population than a set of command keys

EM datasets count **1,315–1,347 descending neurons** and 1,733–1,865 ascending neurons, almost double the light-microscopy estimate of 700 ([Stürner et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12222017/)). Three-quarters of the central brain's output synapses leave through them ([Schlegel et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07686-5)). Optogenetic activation changed behaviour in 91 of 130 descending-neuron lines, and the effects depended on what the fly was already doing ([Cande et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.34275)).

A few neurons behave like commands. **DNg100 (BDN2) and oDN1** start forward walking even in decapitated flies ([Sapkal et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07854-7)). **MDN** drives backward walking ([Bidaye et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1249964)) through several dozen nerve-cord targets ([Feng et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-19936-x)). Many others are "broadcasters." 85% of descending neurons have axon collaterals, mostly in the gnathal ganglia, and command-like neurons recruit other descending neurons there. DNb02 contacts 20 other descending neurons and drives turning in intact flies but only front-leg flexion in headless ones ([Braun et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07523-9)).

Descending neurons operate at high rates and modulate steps rather than switching programmes. The right–left difference in DNa02 activity linearly predicts turning ([Rayshubskiy et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.102230)). DNg13 can exceed 100 spikes/s, and changes in descending activity lead stride changes by about 150 ms. DNa02's stride-locked modulation of about 15 spikes/s is only about 10% of its dynamic range ([Yang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.08.033)). Flight power is population-coded: recruiting more DNg02 cells raises wingbeat amplitude ([Namiki et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2022.01.008)). Imaging about 100 descending neurons per fly found that most encode walking ([Aymanns et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.81527)). Ascending neurons mostly report walking state back to the brain ([Chen et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01281-z)).

Taken together, driving one descending type at about 25 Hz is at the weak end of physiology. A decapitation-proof pathway such as DNg100 or MDN is the right first test of a nerve-cord model.

### Pattern generation lives in each leg's local circuitry

Several lines of evidence now favour per-leg rhythm generators. Amputated leg stumps keep oscillating during walking ([Berendes et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.146720)). A 2026 preprint reports a per-leg central pattern generator whose intrinsic period appears when proprioception is reduced, and attributes interleg coordination to central coupling that feedback and descending input modulate ([Sapkal et al. 2026 preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.29.721658)). Grooming rhythms speed up with temperature, a hallmark of a pattern generator ([Ravbar et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.71508)). Silencing leg sensory neurons degraded step precision but left the tripod gait intact ([Mendes et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.00231)). Strides repeat at about **10 Hz** ([Yang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.08.033)).

The wiring is deep and local. On average, motor-neuron groups receive only **7% of their input directly from descending neurons**, and most descending-to-motor paths cross several layers ([Cheong et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13384506/)). In FANC's front leg, 69 motor neurons receive 212,190 synapses from 1,546 premotor neurons. Local premotor neurons supply about 63% of each motor neuron's input, and descending neurons about 9%. Premotor weights scale with target size, a wiring basis for the size principle of recruitment; wing premotor networks lack this proportionality ([Lesser et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11356479/)). About 120 GABAergic 13A/13B neurons form a flexor burst generator, and pulsed activation "triggers the circuit's intrinsic rhythm rather than pacing it" ([Syed et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.106446)). Physiology adds details a model has to respect. Slow tibia flexor motor neurons fire 12–30 Hz at rest, and a single fast motor-neuron spike can support body weight ([Azevedo et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.56754)). Flight motor neurons fire at only 3–12 Hz, yet weak ShakB coupling makes them fire in ordered splay sequences ([Hürkey et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06099-0)). Activating one neck motor neuron drives the head to a pose specific to that neuron, through a proprioceptive loop ([Gorko et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07222-5)). Amines applied to the nerve cord of decapitated flies trigger walking and grooming ([Yellman et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.94.8.4131)).

### Pugliese's nerve-cord model predicts a rhythm generator, and its recipe is specific

The decisive nerve-cord model is a preprint from the Tuthill and Brunton labs ([Pugliese et al. preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13142387/); [code](https://github.com/smpuglie/Pugliese_cpg_2025)). It simulated front-leg subnetworks of MANC, FANC, BANC and the male CNS as rate units. It used rate units rather than spiking ones because "many neurons in the insect VNC, including premotor neurons active during walking, are nonspiking." Its recipe is specific. Weights are raw synapse counts times one global scale (b = 0.03), with no per-neuron input normalisation. Each neuron's gain is divided, and its threshold multiplied, by its relative size; the authors write that "without adjusting a and θ for size, the network does not produce robust oscillations." Connections under 5 synapses are dropped, glutamate is treated as inhibitory, and every experiment runs across 1,024 replicates with randomised parameters and strong, auto-tuned descending drive.

The results reward that care. **Only about 3–4% of 933 excitatory descending neurons produced robust rhythm**, and DNg100 was the strongest. A single DNb08 neuron produced rhythm in simulation, and optogenetics then confirmed DNb08-driven rhythmic leg movement in decapitated flies (n = 10). Pruning isolated a three-neuron core: excitatory IN17A001 and INXXX466 plus inhibitory IN16B036. It oscillates near 14 Hz, with frequency inversely proportional to τ, survives about 10% weight noise, and recurs in all six legs of all four connectomes. No one has yet recorded from these three interneurons, and the authors say such recordings are still needed, so the core remains a prediction; DNb08 is the confirmed part. An LIF variant with Shiu's parameters on raw counts also produced rhythm.

The failures are equally informative. There was no consistent left–right phase and no coupling among legs, even in the full MANC, and the main tibia flexor motor neurons stayed silent or non-rhythmic. The authors blame missing proprioceptive feedback, mechanical coupling, neuromodulation, co-activation of multiple descending neurons and gap junctions ([Pugliese et al. preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13142387/)).

The recipe's arithmetic explains why fly.ai's nerve cord is silent. With θ/b ≈ 250 synapse-Hz, ten synapses from a descending neuron firing at 25 Hz bring a median-sized Pugliese neuron to threshold. Under sum-to-one normalisation the same edge delivers only its fraction of the target's total input, a derived loss of roughly 100–1,000× per hop for targets with about 1,000 input synapses, which compounds across the several layers between descending and motor neurons.

## Muscle mechanics are measured for barely one motor pool

### Two open bodies exist, and both still move by position targets

Two open MuJoCo fly bodies dominate.

**NeuroMechFly and FlyGym (EPFL).** Version 1 was built from micro-CT, with 7 degrees of freedom per leg and simple spring-damper "muscles" ([Lobato-Rios et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-022-01466-7)). Version 2 added compound eyes of 721 ommatidia each, odour sensors, leg adhesion, rugged terrain and rule-based or pattern-generator controllers. It also added a two-channel "descending" signal that modulates each side's leg oscillators, and it closed a fly-following loop through flyvis run at 500 Hz ([Wang-Chen et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02497-y); [postprint](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf)). FlyGym 2.x (April 2026) is a non-backward-compatible rewrite. It claims about 10× faster CPU simulation (about 2× real time) and about 300× faster GPU simulation (about 60× real time) across batched worlds. Version 2.1 bundles the flybody model and the first muscle-actuated leg, both labelled experimental ([FlyGym](https://github.com/NeLy-EPFL/flygym); [changelog](https://neuromechfly.org/changelog/)).

**flybody (DeepMind and Janelia).** It has 67 body parts, 102 degrees of freedom and a total mass of 0.983 mg, with position actuators on non-wing joints, torque actuators on the wings, quasi-steady ellipsoid aerodynamics, and a wingbeat pattern generator at 218 Hz. Walking and flight are trained by reinforcement learning. On one CPU core, 10 ms of walking takes 68.65 ms and 10 ms of flight takes 421.5 ms. The authors "caution against interpreting the control signals sent to these actuators as biologically meaningful" ([Vaxenburg et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12310536/)).

Every published whole-fly body is still actuated by position or torque targets. Every connectome coupling so far passes through a few descending neurons or a learned decoder, never through motor neurons.

One practical trap is units. FlyGym works in grams, millimetres and seconds, so its force units are µN. Read that way, the "40 mN" adhesion quoted for version 2 comes to about four body weights ([postprint](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf); [mujoco_globals.yaml](https://github.com/NeLy-EPFL/flygym/blob/main/src/flygym/assets/model/neuromechfly/mujoco_globals.yaml)).

### FlyMimic gives one foreleg real muscles

FlyMimic, a conference paper at ICLR 2026, is the first data-driven musculoskeletal fly leg. It has **15 Hill-type muscle-tendon units** on the left foreleg, covering 12 of 19 muscle groups; tibial and tarsal muscles are excluded. Maximum force is cross-sectional area times an assumed 28 mN/mm², a value between the jump muscle (37) and flight muscle (9). Muscle parameters were fitted to walking and grooming kinematics ([Özdil et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06426v1)). The shipped model's muscle names match the connectome's motor-neuron target annotations directly, from tergopleural promotor to tibia flexor. Its activation and deactivation constants, though, are 0.1 and 0.4 ms, about a hundred times faster than MuJoCo's defaults and far faster than measured twitches ([FlyMimic XML](https://github.com/NeLy-EPFL/flygym/blob/main/src/flygym/assets/model/musculoskeletal/best_combined_arm_damping_stiff_cvt3.xml)).

### What has been measured, and what has to be fitted

The connectome side of the motor interface is ready. FANC maps all 69 left front-leg motor neurons to 18 target muscles, using EM, driver lines and X-ray nanotomography ([Azevedo et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11348827/)). MANC identifies all 142 front-leg motor neurons and 198 of 252 middle- and hind-leg motor neurons by serial homology ([Cheong et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13384506/)).

The muscle side is not. Only the tibia flexor pool has good physiology. A slow motor neuron adds **under 0.1 µN per spike**, while a fast one adds **about 10 µN, roughly body weight**. Force reaches half-maximum about 8.5 ms after a fast spike, and the maximum tibia-tip force is about 100 µN ([Azevedo et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.56754)). With all motor neurons silenced, passive joint torques behave like linear springs about **70 times too weak to hold the fly up**, and active force decays with τ ≈ 100 ms ([Wang et al. 2025 preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12324252/)). The only force–velocity data come from the jump muscle: 37 mN/mm² isometric tension and a shortening speed of 6.1 muscle lengths per second at 15 °C ([Eldred et al. 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006349509061414)). A layered walking controller used 10 ms sensory and 30 ms motor delays taken from electrophysiology, and lost robustness when delays exceeded the physiological range ([Karashchuk et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.99005)).

### Flight, neck, proboscis and song have mapped motor neurons but no muscle-level simulation

Flight power comes from asynchronous, stretch-activated muscles that beat at **200–250 Hz** ([Teoh et al. 2025 preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12637562/)). Their motor neurons fire far more slowly, and power rises with firing rate and calcium rather than with recruitment ([Gordon & Dickinson 2006](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.0510109103)). About a dozen steering muscles pair large phasic units with small tonic ones on each hinge sclerite ([Lindsay et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2016.12.018)). A model trained on 72,219 wingbeats of simultaneous muscle imaging and 3-D wing tracking maps steering-muscle activity to wing motion, and a controller over that model reproduces saccades ([Melis et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07293-4)). Haltere afferents make fast electrical synapses onto the b1 steering motor neuron ([Dickerson et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.08.065)). This split has a modelling consequence. Spike-level fidelity matters little for the power muscles. It matters a great deal for steering muscles, whose spikes are phase-locked to a wingbeat of about 4.6 ms.

Elsewhere, the motor neurons are mapped but the mechanics are not. The proboscis has 16 muscles, with genetic access to every one's motor neuron ([McKellar et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.54978)). About 150 abdominal motor neurons in MANC remain unidentified ([Cheong et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13384506/)). Male song reuses the flight motor, with nested pulse and sine pathways ([Lillvis et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.01.015)) and a doublesex-built connection from TN1A interneurons to the sine-song muscle hg1 ([Shirangi et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2016.05.012)). That makes song a natural male-specific target, but no public simulator models song wing motion. A 2026 review adds a warning for all of these layers: parameters that stand for biology "should not be tuned simply to improve the simulation" ([Wang-Chen & Ramdya 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08056)).

## No one has yet closed a validated loop from connectome to body

### Eon's embodied fly is hand-wired by its own account

Eon Systems' March 2026 "embodied fly" is the most publicised attempt, and by its own account a hand-wired one. It joins three existing pieces: Shiu's FlyWire LIF brain, flyvis activations "piped in" as visual input, and NeuroMechFly in MuJoCo, synchronised with the brain every 15 ms ([Eon, 10 Mar 2026](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation)). A handful of outputs drive behaviour. DNa01 and DNa02 steer, oDN1 sets forward speed, MN9 extends the proboscis and antennal descending neurons trigger grooming, all feeding controllers trained by imitation. Looming escape was shown only in the disembodied brain. Eon's own caveats are frank. Brain-to-body mappings were "chosen by hand." Visual inputs are "somewhat 'decorative'" and do not substantially influence behaviour. Internal dynamics have not been checked against ring attractors or rhythm generators. There is no nerve cord, because "motor neurons couldn't be traced since the body wasn't scanned" ([Eon, 8 Mar 2026](https://eon.systems/updates/weve-uploaded-a-fruit-fly)). No embodied-fly code has been released.

The "91%" and "95%" accuracy figures in Eon's posts describe the 2024 disembodied model, not the embodied fly ([Eon, 7 Mar 2026](https://eon.systems/updates/first-multi-behavior-brain-upload); [Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/)). Philip Shiu later called it "not a full blown copy of a fly … maybe we ought to call this a digital twin" ([The Transmitter](https://www.thetransmitter.org/systems-neuroscience/digital-sphinx-raises-questions-about-connectome-models/)). Critics went further. One pointed out that the roughly 15,000-neuron nerve cord is not simulated at all ([Zeleznikow-Johnston, LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ybwcxBRrsKavJB9Wz/no-we-haven-t-uploaded-a-fly-yet)). Patrick Mineault found that most viral fly demos "don't demonstrate a full loop from sensation to motor output." He described the Super Mario 64 demo, Fly64, whose neuron model fly.ai adopted, as "random button mashing." His recommendations were flyvis-style front ends and motor output decoded from the nerve cord ([Mineault, neuroai.science](https://www.neuroai.science/p/are-flies-playing-beat-saber)).

### Trained controllers need null controls, and hobby ports hit fly.ai's walls

The academic record is more modest and more solid. NeuroMechFly v2 closed a loop through a connectome-constrained sub-network in 2024 ([Wang-Chen et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02497-y)). FlyGM trained the FlyWire graph as a fixed operator with learnable per-neuron descriptors to control flybody. Its high-yaw turning error was 8.29, against 13.55 for degree-preserving rewired graphs and 125.36 for random graphs, but those scores measure task performance, not fidelity to fly neurons ([Jin et al. preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17997)). The digital sphinx showed that such controls are essential: a worm connectome with a trained decoder also walks a fly body realistically ([Brunton lab preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.713233v1)).

A 2026 hobby wave has built MaleCNS ports, all unreviewed, and several have already hit the problems fly.ai faces. **Lulzx/fly-brain** runs a 165k-neuron LIF plus flybody and flyvis in a browser, an architecture very close to fly.ai's plan ([GitHub](https://github.com/Lulzx/fly-brain)). **CAOS_FlyCNS** pairs a graded optic lobe, built by mapping flyvis parameters onto MaleCNS wiring, with a spiking central brain and nerve cord ([GitHub](https://github.com/fsantibanezleal/CAOS_FlyCNS)). **flymsg** reports that MaleCNS neurons carry a median 1.81× more synapses than their FlyWire counterparts and rescales Shiu's weight to match, a factor no paper has yet verified ([GitHub](https://github.com/gianlucamazza/flymsg)). **drosophila-brain-mlx** ran MaleCNS with Shiu's unscaled parameters. Taste drove MN9 to 53 Hz, but 69 abdominal neurons exceeded 100 Hz as nerve-cord activity ran away ([GitHub](https://github.com/Kisame76/drosophila-brain-mlx)). **Grigoriy-V/fly-brain** found that transferring flyvis parameters directly onto MaleCNS wiring impaired motion detection. Rescaling gains by input counts partly recovered it, with direction selectivity rising from 0.020 to 0.152 ([GitHub](https://github.com/Grigoriy-V/fly-brain)).

### The worm and zebrafish show what the fly still lacks: a shared benchmark

Other animals show the pattern. Worm emulation restarted only when multi-compartment neurons were fitted to functional data and coupled to a 96-muscle soft body ([BAAIWorm, Nat Comput Sci 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-024-00738-w)). Zebrafish has the model benchmark the fly lacks: ZAPBench scores forecasts of 71,721 recorded neurons, and the same fish's connectome is in progress ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/improving-brain-models-with-zapbench/)).

Optimistic roadmaps put a fly emulation within this decade at a cost in the low hundreds of millions of dollars, with data acquisition as the binding constraint ([State of Brain Emulation Report](https://brainemulation.mxschons.com/)). That report's co-authors include Eon's head of engineering ([The Transmitter](https://www.thetransmitter.org/systems-neuroscience/digital-sphinx-raises-questions-about-connectome-models/)). No source offers an evidence-based timeline for a *validated* emulation, and there is no agreed fly benchmark.

## fly.ai's failures trace to a handful of modelling choices

### fly.ai runs all 166,700 neurons as identical, normalised LIF units

fly.ai simulates the whole MaleCNS v1.0: **166,700 neurons and 25,582,938 connected neuron pairs**. That includes 89,403 optic-lobe intrinsic neurons, 13,161 nerve-cord intrinsic neurons, 708 motor neurons and 1,314 descending neurons ([fly.ai experiments](../research_notes/Embodied%20fly%20connectome%20simulation/project_experiments.md)). Every neuron is an identical LIF unit following Fly64, with **τ = 100 ms**, threshold 1 and reset 0, stepped at **20 ms** (2 ms optional), plus a tonic drive of 0.14 per step, a global gain of 3.0 and Poisson noise. Weights are synapse counts, made negative for GABA, glutamate and histamine, and **normalised so each neuron's absolute inputs sum to one**. As of 25 September 2026, opt-in per-type parameters and a graded mode exist. With the new options off, the model reproduces the original bit for bit ([fly.ai experiments](../research_notes/Embodied%20fly%20connectome%20simulation/project_experiments.md)). The main visual route skips the eye: hand-built feature detectors drive LPLC2, LC4, LPLC1 and LC10a directly. The README states plainly that none of this is validated against recordings: "It's a demo, not an emulation" ([fly.ai README](../README.md)). The README also documents why the original Fly64 settings were abandoned. A tonic drive of 0.18 with decay e^−0.2 parks every neuron exactly at threshold, so the network ticks at about 4 Hz whatever the fly sees ([fly.ai README](../README.md)).

### What works: lateralised shortcuts past the eye

Injecting activity downstream of the eye produces correct, lateralised responses. Driving LC4 and LPLC2 on one side raises the same-side giant fiber (DNp01) by **17–25 Hz**. Driving LC10a raises the same-side steering neuron DNa02 by 1.4–3.7 Hz, and the opposite side does not move ([fly.ai experiments](../research_notes/Embodied%20fly%20connectome%20simulation/project_experiments.md)). This is real but weak evidence. Both are short, heavily weighted feedforward routes that simple graph-traversal models also predict.

The two-fly "song" experiment is more nuanced than its headline. At 20 ms steps, after the smell fix, songs carried 0.63 bits about the singer's situation against 0.01 bits for scrambled wiring. At 2 ms, the real brain carried 0.83 bits and the scrambled brain 0.87. At 2 ms the listener reacted to threat songs 77.5% of the time against 17.5% for silence, but it used only loudness: a time-shuffled song worked as well ([fly.ai README](../README.md)).

### The eye path: correct signs, weak signal

The original model stopped light at the first synapse. A looming object moved photoreceptors by −7 Hz, but L1 moved +0.1 Hz and LC4, LPLC2 and DNp01 did not move at all. That is exactly what histamine inhibition onto silent spiking units predicts.

With graded photoreceptors and lamina, the pre-registered test failed: no setting raised the LC4/LPLC2 population mean by 3 Hz. Yet the biology came out right in sign and side. L1 rose 3.1 Hz-equivalent and L2 1.5. The OFF channel rose (Tm1 +0.5 Hz, Tm2 +1.0 Hz, T5 +0.9 Hz) while the ON channel (Mi1, T4) fell, as it should for a dark object. LPLC2 rose 1.2–1.5 Hz and LC4 0.4–0.8 Hz, on the stimulated side only. The response was retinotopically concentrated. The top tenth of LPLC2 rose 4.7 Hz and the top tenth of T5 rose 6.2 Hz, against real looming responses of tens of hertz ([fly.ai experiments](../research_notes/Embodied%20fly%20connectome%20simulation/project_experiments.md)).

Five compounding causes explain the remaining shortfall. The first is **missing photoreceptors**: only 3,377 R1–6 cells exist in MaleCNS, about 32% of those expected, and 66% of left lamina L1–L3 neurons receive no photoreceptor input at all ([fly.ai experiments](../research_notes/Embodied%20fly%20connectome%20simulation/project_experiments.md)), consistent with the reconstruction's documented edge losses ([Nern et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08746-0)). Second, **normalisation dilutes what remains**, since photoreceptors supply only 7–16% of lamina neurons' normalised input. Third, **one untuned graded gain** saturates half the units when raised, whereas flyvis fits a resting potential and time constant per type. Fourth, the model's τ and step ignore the **20–70 ms filtering timescales** of photoreceptors and medulla. Fifth, the **population-mean criterion** averages over cells whose receptive fields never saw the stimulus. The giant fiber's small 0.3 Hz rise is consistent with the weak LPLC2 input, given how strongly direct injection drives it.

### The nerve cord: commands that never arrive

Driving DNg100, DNa02, DNp01 or MDN at about 25 Hz moved no motor-neuron group by more than 0.6 Hz. Raising nerve-cord tonic drive and gain, or amplifying only descending-to-premotor and premotor-to-motor synapses, failed every pre-registered criterion ([fly.ai experiments](../research_notes/Embodied%20fly%20connectome%20simulation/project_experiments.md)). The literature points to several causes acting at once. **Fan-in dilution** from sum-to-one normalisation costs about two to three orders of magnitude per hop compared with Pugliese's raw-count recipe. The model has **no size-scaled excitability**, which Pugliese found "crucial" for oscillation. The **membrane is too slow**: rhythm frequency scales with 1/τ, so a τ of 100 ms would push a 7–15 Hz stepping rhythm down to roughly 1.5–3 Hz, and at 20 ms steps a real stride spans only three to four steps. The **drive is weak**, since 25 Hz is far below both physiological descending rates and the drive in working models. The model lacks the **tonic proprioceptive input** on which the real cord idles, where slow motor neurons fire at 12–30 Hz. Finally, some **tests were poorly chosen**. DNp01's output to the jump and flight muscles runs through gap junctions the model lacks, DNa02 modulates strides rather than starting them, and MDN depends on the leg rhythm generator. DNg100 and DNb08 are the right first tests.

### Scrambled wiring and a hot resting state

The scramble result has a structural explanation. Under sum-to-one normalisation, any sign-preserving rewiring leaves every neuron's total input weight unchanged. Uniform tonic drive then makes gross activity depend on degree, not wiring, which is the larval-connectome finding ([Therianos preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17745)). Routing-specific tests should separate real from scrambled wiring. Candidates are lateralised loom-to-giant-fiber, which fly.ai has not yet run against a scramble, and sugar-to-MN9, which fails in 99 of 100 weight shuffles in Shiu's model ([Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/)).

Resting activity is too hot. Central-brain intrinsic neurons idle at about 12 Hz and the non-optic neurons average 8.35 Hz, against a metabolic estimate of about 4 Hz as the ceiling on mean firing ([State of Brain Emulation Report 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15745)). Olfactory receptor neurons ran away because they received 0.43 of their 0.45 net input from each other ([fly.ai README](../README.md)). The real antennal lobe absorbs about 20,000 spontaneous receptor spikes per second through presynaptic gain control and short-term depression ([Olsen & Wilson 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06864); [Nagel et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3895)), and the model has neither.

## Nine rungs to an embodied fly, each gated by real-fly data and a null control

The literature suggests an order of work. Anchor to the one recipe with biological validation, then replace one layer at a time with the unit type and data the biology demands. No rung counts as passed unless the effect degrades under scrambled wiring.

### Four habits keep every rung honest

First, **use biological timescales**: run spiking units at sub-millisecond steps (the reference Shiu reimplementations use 0.1 ms), not at 20 ms, so that 1.8 ms delays and 2.2 ms refractory periods mean something. Second, **score routing, not gross activity**. Pre-registered criteria should test the correct side, the correct target against matched decoys, dose-response, latency order and cessation after the stimulus, the checklist one MaleCNS port already uses ([flymsg](https://github.com/gianlucamazza/flymsg)). Third, **climb a null ladder**: weight shuffle, then degree-preserving rewiring, then degree- and weight-matched ensembles, then cell-class-preserving rewiring. Anything with a trained decoder also needs a non-fly connectome and noise-only drive, the controls that the digital sphinx and FlyGM made unavoidable ([digital sphinx](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.713233v1); [FlyGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17997)). Fourth, **test robustness to wiring variability** by repeating key results with only conserved edges, those above 10 synapses or 1% of input ([Schlegel et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07686-5)), and with type-matched FlyWire or BANC wiring.

Parameters should be shared by cell type and fitted as ensembles, not hand-tuned to a single point. Theory shows that recordings from a few well-chosen types remove most of the degeneracy ([Beiran & Litwin-Kumar 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-02080-4)). Differentiable simulators make such fits practical at this scale ([Jaxley](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02895-w); [Wang et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12913608/)).

### Sub-millisecond steps are affordable; fitting is the expensive part

Moving to biological timescales has a compute cost. At 20 ms steps, fly.ai needs 12–15 ms of wall time per step on 24 threads ([fly.ai README](../README.md)). A 0.1 ms step multiplies the step count by 200, so real-time runs need GPU or fused-kernel code. A Shiu-parameter MaleCNS port already runs at 1.17 s per biological second on a single Apple M4 Pro machine ([drosophila-brain-mlx, unreviewed](https://github.com/Kisame76/drosophila-brain-mlx)).

The body is not the bottleneck. Both body models already step at 0.05–0.1 ms, so lockstep coupling is natural ([FlyGym](https://github.com/NeLy-EPFL/flygym); [Vaxenburg et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12310536/)). Eon's 15 ms brain–body sync is longer than the 8.5 ms twitch rise it would need to resolve ([Eon](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation); [Azevedo et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.56754)). FlyGym's advertised GPU speed-ups come from batching many identical worlds. They will not accelerate one fly coupled to one brain.

The expensive part is fitting. An online spiking-gradient rule fits the whole brain in about 8.9 GB of GPU memory, while backpropagation through time exceeds 32 GB ([Wang et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12913608/)).

### Data gaps will cap how far validation can reach

No whole-brain activity atlas is indexed to connectome neuron IDs, so whole-brain imaging can be compared with a model only at the level of regions and populations ([Schaffer et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10495430/); [Brezovec et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38242122/)). Nearly all of those datasets come from females, which leaves MaleCNS's male-specific predictions untested unless they are checked against BANC or FlyWire. Imaging is also too slow for spike timing: even the fastest whole-brain method, at 28 volumes per second, tests population dynamics rather than spikes ([Gauthey et al. 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72437-1)). No public dataset pairs motor-neuron spikes with muscle force and joint kinematics in behaving flies; the closest is single-motor-neuron force recording in tethered animals ([Azevedo et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.56754)). And three reference maps simply do not exist yet: an electrical-synapse map, a spiking-status database and a whole-CNS receptor-sign map. Each missing resource marks a layer where the model's claims must stay provisional.

## Conclusion

The fly's own design dictates a heterogeneous model. Early vision is graded and sign-inverting. Motor patterning is local, recurrent and size-ordered. The fastest escape relays are electrical. Control is distributed toward the body part being moved. A network built from one neuron type and one gain therefore fails in predictable places, wherever the biology departs from the short spiking chains that Shiu validated. fly.ai's failures are, in effect, independent rediscoveries of documented limits. Disinhibition is invisible without a baseline. The nerve cord does not oscillate without size-scaled excitability. Gross activity is set by degree statistics, not by exact wiring. Read that way, the failures are a map of which refinements the evidence requires rather than a sign that the approach is broken. The largest near-term gain will come less from added realism than from sharper tests. A routing-specific result that scrambled wiring fails is worth more than a larger response that it matches.

The strategic opening is that MaleCNS contains the nerve cord, the complete male gustatory system and the song circuitry. That lets fly.ai attempt what Eon could not: move a body through its motor neurons rather than through hand-picked command shortcuts, and test male-specific behaviours against female connectomes as controls. The binding constraint has shifted from wiring to physiology: muscle mechanics beyond one motor pool, electrical synapses, receptor-level signs and activity registered to identified neurons. An embodied fly that states, layer by layer, which parts are validated, which are fitted and which are still guesses would already be something the 2026 wave of embodied demos has not offered.
