# Drosophila sensory periphery: transduction, parameters, and realistic inputs for a connectome-based simulated fly

Notes compiled 2026-09-25. "Preprint" = not peer-reviewed at time of writing; everything else is peer-reviewed unless flagged. Numbers are quoted with their conditions (temperature, adaptation state, dataset). Where a number came from a search-engine summary rather than the primary text, this is flagged.

## 1. Vision: eye geometry, photoreceptors, lamina, existing models, graded dynamics, and relaying light through a spiking network

### Takeaway
The fly eye samples roughly 750-900 directions depending on sex/dataset (about 750 ommatidia in female micro-CT maps, about 800 columns in FAFB, 892 medulla columns in the MaleCNS right optic lobe) with inter-ommatidial angles of about 4.5-5 deg and R1-6 acceptance angles of about 8-9.5 deg (dark) that narrow with light adaptation and photomechanical microsaccades. Photoreceptors are graded, strongly adaptive cells (impulse time-to-peak about 40 ms dim to 20 ms bright at 25 C) that release histamine onto sign-inverting chloride channels (Ort/HisCl1) of graded lamina neurons. Every working visual-system model (flyvis, NeuroMechFly v2 + flyvis, Borst-style motion models) treats retina, lamina and medulla as graded rate units. A leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) network with one global parameter set cannot relay light through the inhibitory R-to-L synapse unless lamina and medulla neurons get tonic drive or the optic lobe is run as a graded front end whose outputs are converted to spike rates for the central brain.

### Cited Findings

#### Eye geometry and sampling
- A fruit-fly eye has about 750 ommatidia on an approximate hemisphere. Inter-ommatidial spacing is smallest frontally near the equator and increases away from it; vertical spacing is tightest along the equator and horizontal spacing tightest frontally. Field of view runs from directly above down to -70 deg elevation, and in azimuth from <10 deg into the opposite hemisphere anteriorly to about 155 deg posteriorly; binocular overlap is <20 deg; there is a posterior blind spot of about 50 deg. Maps were consistent across micro-CT scans of three females; eye-to-medulla mapping used 779 Mi1 columns from the FAFB right optic lobe. Numeric regional angle values are in Extended Data (not extracted here); no public URL for the viewing-direction data was found in the article text — [Zhao et al. 2025, Nature, "Eye structure shapes neuron function in Drosophila motion vision"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12488493/) (published version of a 2022 bioRxiv preprint; [Nature page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09276-5)).
- Lens diameter 16.85 +/- 0.21 um with no regional specialization; smallest inter-ommatidial angle laterally 4.5 +/- 0.08 deg; dark-adapted R1-6 acceptance angle 8.23 +/- 0.54 deg (ratio about 1.77, "consistent with a crepuscular ecology"); dark-adapted latency 6.2 +/- 0.8 ms; reliable encoding up to about 43 Hz; about 260 bits/s information rate; recordings at 19 C — [Gonzalez-Bellido, Wardill & Juusola 2011, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3054003/).
- R1-6 receptive-field half-widths: 9.47 +/- 1.57 deg dark-adapted (n = 19) and 7.70 +/- 1.27 deg light-adapted (n = 6); narrowest light-adapted cell 5.73 deg. Photomechanical rhabdomere microsaccades move and transiently narrow receptive fields (modelled as 8.1 to 4.0 deg; 8.2-9.5 to 3.5-4.5 deg), enabling hyperacuity. Average inter-ommatidial angle is about 4.5 deg (Gotz 1964; Land 1997). R1-R6 from six neighbouring ommatidia view the same point (neural superposition), improving SNR by up to sqrt(6) — [Juusola et al. 2017, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/26117).
- Frontal left and right photoreceptors show mirror-symmetric microsaccades that encode binocular disparity (hyperacute 3D vision) — [Kemppainen et al. 2022, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2109717119).
- Male vs female column counts: the MaleCNS optic lobes have about 900 columns vs about 800 in FAFB. The MaleCNS paper normalises female counts by x1.134 "to account for the ~100 additional ommatidia in the male eye" and attributes much of the difference to biological variability — [Berg et al. MaleCNS preprint (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/). The male right optic lobe was assigned 892 hexagonal medulla coordinates (Mi1: 887; T4a/b/c/d: 849/846/883/860) — [Nern et al. 2025 (preprint version in PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11042306/). In FlyWire (female) the likely true column count is about 800 (lower bound of the estimated range 720) — [Matsliah et al. 2024, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446827/). Contradiction: females are usually larger, yet this male has more columns than FAFB; treat column number as an individual-level parameter.

#### Photoreceptor classes and spectral sensitivity
- First in vivo spectral measurements: Rh3 peak about 330 nm, Rh4 about 355 nm, Rh5 about 435 nm, Rh6 about 600 nm (shifted 92 nm from the long-cited 508 nm and broadened), Rh1 dual peaks about 360 and 490 nm — [Sharkey et al. 2020, Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-74742-1) (values from indexed text summary).
- Yellow- and pale-type ommatidia are stochastically arranged at a 7:3 ratio; a third, dorsal-rim type is specialised for polarization — [NeuroMechFly v2 postprint](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf).
- Inner photoreceptors R7 (UV-preferring) and R8 (blue/green-preferring) are more narrowly tuned than R1-6 and project to the medulla, not the lamina. Tm5 neurons (six connectomic types) are candidate R7/R8 targets; the canonical Tm5 types were originally identified by Ort (histamine receptor) expression — [Matsliah et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446827/). In the MaleCNS, pale vs yellow columns were assigned from R7 connectivity with Dm8a/Tm5a (yellow) vs Dm8b/Tm5b (pale) and aMe12 markers — [MaleCNS preprint](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/).

#### Phototransduction models and parameters
- Stochastic microvillus model: a R1-R6 rhabdomere has about 30,000 microvilli, each an independent stochastic G-protein cascade that can produce only one quantum bump at a time and is then refractory (100-200 ms, stochastically variable); 5-15 TRP/TRPL channels open per bump; Ca2+-calmodulin feedback on multiple targets; Hodgkin-Huxley K+ membrane whose voltage reduces cation driving force; photons are distributed stochastically across microvilli. Validated against intracellular recordings at 25 C with white-noise and naturalistic stimuli (light pulses 3,000 to 3x10^5 photons/s). Residual mismatch attributed to omitted pupil, synaptic feedback and slow adaptation. Code availability not stated — [Song et al. 2012, Curr Biol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3420010/).
- Follow-up biomimetic model: refractory period 50-300 ms; light adaptation emerges from four constraints (microvillus number, Poisson photon absorption, refractoriness, stochastic bump latency/waveform) — [Song & Juusola 2017, J Physiol](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1113/JP273614). Contradiction: refractory range reported as 100-200 ms (2012) vs 50-300 ms (2017).
- Measured R1-6 parameters at 25 C: dark membrane time constant about 20 ms; input resistance 700 +/- 540 MOhm (dark) and 320 +/- 100 MOhm (light-adapted, depolarised 25-40 mV); capacitance 52 +/- 18 pF; bump duration falls from about 50 ms (dim, BG-4 = about 300 effective photons/s) to about 10 ms (BG0 = about 3x10^6 photons/s); dead-time from about 25 to about 10 ms; impulse-response time-to-peak halves from about 40 to about 20 ms; information capacity saturates at about 200-300 bits/s near 3x10^5 photons/s — [Juusola & Hardie 2001a, J Gen Physiol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2232468/).
- Temperature: time-to-peak falls from >40 ms (<18 C) to 13 ms (30 C), Q10 = 2.5 +/- 0.2; dead-time from >20 ms (<20 C) to about 5 ms (30 C), Q10 = 3.1; information rate >400 bits/s at 29 C; at 18 C a 25-ms dead-time caps each microvillus at about 40 photons/s, i.e. 30,000 microvilli handle at most about 1.2x10^6 photons/s — [Juusola & Hardie 2001b, J Gen Physiol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2232470/).
- Information-rate figures differ by temperature and study: about 260 bits/s at 19 C ([Gonzalez-Bellido 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3054003/)), 200-300 bits/s at 25 C and >400 bits/s at 29 C ([Juusola & Hardie 2001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2232470/)). A tool summary of Song 2012 gave about 350 bits/s for Drosophila; this was not verified in the primary text.

#### Lamina, histamine and sign inversion
- Histamine released by photoreceptors directly gates chloride channels on large monopolar cells (LMCs), so LMCs respond to light with a rapid chloride-mediated hyperpolarization. Two genes contribute: hclA (= ort) and hclB (= HisCl1). ort-null flies are behaviourally blind with no ERG ON/OFF transients; HisCl1 mutants show larger OFF transients and slower LMC responses — [Pantazis et al. 2008, J Neurosci](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/28/29/7250) (from indexed summary). HisCl1 was first localised to lamina glia; later work found it in photoreceptors, particularly R7/R8 — [Alejevski et al. 2019, Nat Commun](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-08116-7).
- R1-R6 output is pooled and sent through histaminergic synapses to LMCs and amacrine cells, which feed back onto photoreceptor terminals. In vivo, LMC resting potentials were -40 to -70 mV and light pulses evoked transient hyperpolarizations of up to 45 mV; some hyperpolarizing responses probably came from amacrine cells; synaptic feedback increases photoreceptor information transfer — [Zheng et al. 2006, J Gen Physiol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2151524/).
- Male optic-lobe lamina reconstruction is incomplete: a "stair-step profile due to the trimmed edges of the 20 um slabs" and heavily stained severed photoreceptor axons; lamina connection completeness about 47% vs 54.2% for the rest of the optic lobe; R1-6 and Lai counts are underestimates; some R7/R8 could not be identified — [Nern et al. 2025 (PMC preprint version)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11042306/); [Nature version](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08746-0). The MaleCNS paper: "some R1-6 photoreceptor neurons in the laminae" could not be reconstructed because of sample edge artefacts, and "Photoreceptors and Lai ... many are missing" — [MaleCNS preprint](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/).
- In FlyWire v783, R7 and R8 number about 650 each and R1-6 total about 3,400 (photoreceptors are under-recovered) — [Matsliah et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446827/).

#### Existing models: flyvis, NeuroMechFly v2, flybody and others
- flyvis (Lappalainen et al. 2024): 45,669 neurons, 1,513,231 connections, 64 cell types on a 721-column hexagonal lattice (31 columns across) modelling the central field of the "700-900 ommatidia". Dynamics: tau_t dV_i/dt = -V_i + sum_j s_ij + V_rest,t + e_i, with graded synapses s_ij = alpha * sigma * N_syn * f(V_j), f = ReLU. Only 734 free parameters (65 time constants, 65 resting potentials, 604 synapse scale factors). Time constants initialised at 50 ms and clamped to >= dt; dt = 20 ms (5 ms for some protocols); resting potentials drawn from N(0.5, 0.05) a.u. Photoreceptor input = mean grey value of a 13x13-pixel patch per hexagon, 8 photoreceptor types (R1-R8), neural superposition ignored; R8 output was treated as hyperpolarizing or depolarizing depending on whether the target type is known to express the histamine receptor gene. Connectome from FIB-25 (7 medulla columns) plus FIB-19 (1,801 neurons total), assuming perfect periodicity. Ensemble of 50 networks trained on optic flow (23 Sintel sequences, 24 fps). No electrical synapses, nonlinear chemical synapses or neuromodulation. All neurons are non-spiking single compartments (CT1 has one compartment per column) — [Lappalainen et al. 2024, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11525180/); code and pretrained ensembles at [TuragaLab/flyvis](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flyvis) ([docs](https://turagalab.github.io/flyvis/)); latest release v1.2.0 on 2026-08-06 ([releases](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flyvis/releases)).
- NeuroMechFly v2 vision: a colour camera per eye is transformed into 721 hexagonal bins, assuming 270 deg combined azimuth and about 17 deg binocular overlap. A fisheye correction is applied (r* = (r/zeta)/(1 - k(r^2 + c^2)), k = 3.8, zeta = 2.72) and pixels are averaged per ommatidium. Yellow- and pale-type ommatidia read the green and blue render channels (users can substitute reflectance x spectral-sensitivity products); the dorsal rim area is not implemented. The observation is 2 eyes x 721 x 2 channels. For fly-following, flyvis was run frame by frame in closed loop at 500 Hz (the eye sampling rate), eyes independently, using the single best model; T1-T5, Tm and TmY outputs (the "34 T-shaped transmedullary" output types) were read out and compared with baseline to detect objects (z-score threshold chi_thr = 7) — [Wang-Chen et al. 2024, Nature Methods (postprint)](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf); [journal page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02497-y).
- FlyGym 2.x (released March/April 2026; v2.1.0 on 2026-06-24) is a full rewrite with about 10x CPU and about 300x GPU (MJWarp) speed-ups. It is not backward compatible and "not all features from FlyGym 1.x.x are available"; 1.x lives on as flygym-gymnasium — [FlyGym README](https://github.com/NeLy-EPFL/flygym); [releases](https://github.com/NeLy-EPFL/flygym/releases). Which 1.x vision/olfaction features exist in 2.x was not verified.
- flybody (MuJoCo): female whole-body model with 66 joints / 102 DoFs, eye cameras downsampled to greyscale frames for vision-guided flight, and adhesion actuators; the authors caution against reading position-actuator commands as biological — [Vaxenburg et al. 2025, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12310536/).
- A 2026 GitHub project ports flyvis parameters onto MaleCNS wiring (60 types, 721 columns, 31,526 units, 1.35M edges). It reports that direct transfer impaired motion detection and that rescaling synaptic gain by input counts recovered it (direction selectivity 0.020 to 0.152), with an OFF-pathway spatial defect remaining. Not peer-reviewed — [Grigoriy-V/fly-brain](https://github.com/Grigoriy-V/fly-brain).
- Male-connectome visual pathway analysis traced photoreceptor signals through the optic lobes into the central brain — [Cell 2026, "The organization of visual pathways in the Drosophila brain"](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00941-4) (summary only; full text not accessed).

#### Graded-potential filters used for non-spiking optic-lobe neurons
- Whole-cell filters measured with 50%-contrast Gaussian flicker (10 ms correlation time): peak response time Mi1 71 +/- 3.8 ms, Tm3 53 +/- 5.2 ms (ON, L1 pathway), Tm1 56 +/- 3.8 ms, Tm2 43 +/- 2.7 ms (OFF, L2 pathway). The filters are band-pass with a main lobe plus a shallow opposite lobe, followed by partial half-wave rectification; Mi1's offset hyperpolarization is 11% of onset (Tm3: 36.6%) — [Behnia et al. 2014, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4243710/).
- Mi9 and Mi4 are slow, sustained low-pass; Mi1 and Tm3 are fast, transient band-pass — [Arenz et al. 2017, Curr Biol](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982217300866), as summarised in [Borst 2018, PLoS Comput Biol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6016951/). Borst's T4/T5 model uses a first-order high-pass (tau = 250 ms, plus 10% DC) for the central Mi1/Tm3-like input and first-order low-pass (tau = 50 ms) for flanking Mi4/Mi9-like inputs, 40x40 detectors spanning 180 x 180 deg (4.5 deg each) after a 4.5-deg Gaussian blur, 10 ms time step — [Borst 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6016951/).

#### Spiking and LIF approaches to visual input
- Shiu et al. LIF whole-brain model (FlyWire): V_rest = V_reset = -52 mV, V_th = -45 mV, tau_m = 20 ms, synaptic tau = 5 ms, refractory 2.2 ms, delay 1.8 ms, w_syn = 0.275 mV per synapse; stimulated neurons receive Poisson input (default 150 Hz, weight scaled by f_poi = 250) with no refractory period — [philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model model.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model/main/model.py). The model predicted activation phenotypes of an unbiased sample of cell types at >90% accuracy; the circuits tested were gustatory (feeding) and mechanosensory (antennal grooming), not visual — [Shiu et al. 2024, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/). A multi-backend port (Brian2, Brian2CUDA, PyTorch, NEST GPU, GeNN; 0.1 ms step; FlyWire v783) stimulates neurons with Poisson "optogenetic" activation and had no body coupling at time of access — [eonsystemspbc/fly-brain](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain).
- A 2026 LIF optic-lobe model on FAFB (described by the authors as 138,639 neurons) skipped photoreceptors entirely and "applied input directly to L1-L3" as Poisson trains whose rates depend on bar position relative to ommatidium centres (from calcium-imaging data); the authors state it "lacks in vivo validation" — [Liew et al. 2026, arXiv (preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01330).
- Recent embodied or connectome-controller work includes a "Fly-connectomic Graph Model" that uses the whole-brain connectome graph as an RL-trained controller for a simulated fly body; sensory handling was not described in the abstract — [Jin et al. 2026, arXiv (preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17997).

### Inferences
- The eye model must match the connectome, not flyvis. For MaleCNS, map its roughly 892 medulla columns (column assignments in the Reiser-lab code: [reiserlab/male-drosophila-visual-system-connectome-code](https://github.com/reiserlab/male-drosophila-visual-system-connectome-code)) to viewing directions. Zhao et al.'s micro-CT and kernel-regression approach gives a female template to warp. The 721-hexagon flyvis/NeuroMechFly lattice only covers the central field, so resample the render to the full column set rather than truncating.
- Photoreceptor front end, cheapest adequate option: per-column divisive (Weber-like) adaptation plus a linear filter with a 10-25 ms dead-time and a 20-40 ms time-to-peak, scaled for temperature (Q10 about 2.5-3). Use the 30,000-microvillus stochastic model only for validation, since running it for about 5,000+ photoreceptors is expensive. Treat R7/R8 as separate spectral channels feeding the medulla directly; they bypass the lamina.
- Why input "dies" at the lamina in a single-parameter LIF: histaminergic R-to-L1/L2/L3 synapses are inhibitory, and silent LIF neurons cannot be inhibited further. Biologically, LMCs are graded and depolarised at rest, and light is signalled as hyperpolarization. The sign is restored one synapse later: the literature reports L1 as glutamatergic (acting through inhibitory GluCl on Mi1/Tm3, giving ON responses) and L2 as cholinergic (giving OFF responses in Tm1/Tm2). This was not verified in this session, so check it against MaleCNS transmitter predictions. Three workable designs:
  (a) Give graded optic-lobe types a tonic bias current (the LIF analogue of flyvis' per-type resting potentials) so they fire at baseline in darkness and light modulates them bidirectionally. Silencing that bias in the dark-adapted state reproduces the current failure.
  (b) Hybrid: run the retina, lamina and medulla (or the whole optic lobe) as flyvis-style graded units (dt 5-20 ms, or 2 ms to match NeuroMechFly's 500 Hz), then convert visual projection neuron (or T4/T5, LC, LPLC) activities into Poisson rates for the spiking central brain. NeuroMechFly v2 already runs flyvis at 500 Hz in closed loop.
  (c) Bypass: inject rate-coded input directly into L1-L3 (as Liew et al. did) or further downstream. This is simplest but discards photoreceptor adaptation and the R7/R8 pathway.
- Timing sanity check: photoreceptor dead-time (10-25 ms) plus medulla filter peaks (43-71 ms) put visual responses at projection neurons tens of ms after the stimulus. A LIF chain with 1.8 ms synaptic delays reaches the optic-lobe outputs far too fast unless explicit temporal filters or graded stages are added.
- Where the lamina is missing or partial (MaleCNS/BANC), synthesise R1-6 input per column onto the reconstructed L1-L3, or inject at L1-L3. Photoreceptor-dependent validation should use FAFB, whose lamina is present, although its photoreceptors are also under-recovered.

### Gaps
- Regional inter-ommatidial angle values and a downloadable ommatidia-direction table (Zhao 2025) were not obtained; no male-specific eye map was found.
- No verified, complete table of which optic-lobe types spike and which are graded. Several visual projection neurons are widely reported to spike, but this was not sourced here.
- Drosophila-specific LMC linear filters (time-to-peak, high-pass time constants) were not extracted; Zheng 2006 gives amplitudes, not filter kernels.
- Code availability for the Song/Juusola stochastic microvillus model is unconfirmed.
- Exact counts of R1-6, L1-L5, amacrine and Lawf neurons present in MaleCNS v1.0 were not queried from neuPrint.

## 2. Olfaction: receptor-response databases, ORN dynamics, ORN-to-PN transformation, glomerulus mapping and plume simulation

### Takeaway
Use DoOR 2.0 (693 odorants x 78 responding units, 7,381 data points, normalised 0-1, glomerulus-mapped) as the static receptor-by-odorant table, anchored by Hallem & Carlson 2006's empty-neuron recordings. For dynamics, the best-supported ORN model has two stages: (1) ligand-receptor kinetics with adaptive feedback (the transduction/LFP stage), then (2) a stereotyped biphasic, differentiating filter into spike rate (Nagel & Wilson 2011), with fast Weber-like gain control over roughly 0.3-0.5 s (Gorur-Shandilya 2017). ORN-to-PN transmission is a saturating hyperbolic ratio with exponent 1.5 plus divisive lateral input-gain control (Olsen 2010); each ORN spike gives a PN a unitary EPSP of about 6 mV (Kazama & Wilson 2008). In connectomes, ORN types are named by glomerulus (58 glomeruli incl. 7 thermo/hygro in the hemibrain analysis). NeuroMechFly v2 supplies a PhiFlow 2D plume and an encounter-driven navigation model.

### Cited Findings
- DoOR 2.0 merges all available odorant-response data into one consensus matrix: 693 odorants, 78 responding units (receptors/ORN classes), 7,381 data points. Data from different methods are rescaled to [0,1], merged by pairwise monotonic fits (five function types) and globally normalised. The spontaneous firing rate is treated as an odorant and subtracted, which preserves inhibitory responses. Nearly all responding units are assigned glomeruli (exception VA7m). Limitations: no concentration dependence, no temporal dynamics, no mixtures, and saturation distorts the mapping functions. Web interface http://neuro.uni.kn/DoOR; R packages DoOR.data and DoOR.functions on GitHub/CRAN/Zenodo — [Munch & Galizia 2016, Sci Rep](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4766438/).
- Hallem & Carlson 2006 used the "empty neuron" system (an antennal neuron lacking its endogenous receptor, into which single receptors are expressed) with "a panel of over 100 odors". Strong responses are sparse and depend on chemical class; receptors range from narrowly to broadly tuned; inhibitory responses are widespread; temporal dynamics carry odour quality, quantity and duration; receptors with similar sensitivity map to widely dispersed glomeruli — [Hallem & Carlson 2006, Cell (abstract)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16615896/).
- ORN dynamics split into transduction and spike generation. Transduction is largely explained by simple ligand-receptor kinetics plus an adaptive feedback that slows onset. Spiking is a differentiating biphasic linear filter, stereotyped across odours and cells; Na+ channel knock-down makes it more purely differentiating (peak-to-steady-state ratio 1.7-2.3). The stimulus-to-LFP filter lobe has a 105 ms half-width, so the LFP follows odour fluctuations up to about 6 Hz (20 dB attenuation), with absolute latency <10 ms and LFP event rise times of 28-32 ms. Spontaneous rates were 12.8 +/- 2.6 spikes/s in control ORNs vs 36.4 +/- 17.9 with ectopic OR47b — [Nagel & Wilson 2011, Nat Neurosci](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3030680/).
- ORN gain changes dynamically with naturalistic stimuli. Response deviations fall with the mean stimulus over the preceding 300 ms (firing rate: Spearman rho = -0.68) and are uncorrelated with whiff amplitude. Transduction can be adapted by pulses as short as 35 ms on timescales as fast as 500 ms (citing Cao et al. 2016). Natural-stimulus mean and SD are most correlated over about 400 ms windows. Transduction currents scale inversely with background (Weber-Fechner) — [Gorur-Shandilya et al. 2017, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/27670).
- Behaviour-level stand-in for the ORN population ("adaptive compression then filtering", fitted to walking flies with ethanol): Hill dissociation constants of 0.072% (ON) and 0.127% (OFF), Hill coefficients about 1 (1.03/1.06). ON model: tau_ON = 0.72 s, adaptation tau_A = 9.8 s. OFF model: tau_OFF1 = 0.62 s, tau_OFF2 = 4.84 s, tau_A = 10.0 s. Adaptation is implemented as a slow rightward shift of the Hill midpoint — [Alvarez-Salvado et al. 2018, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/37815).
- ORN-to-PN transformation: PN = Rmax * ORN^1.5 / (ORN^1.5 + sigma^1.5) within a glomerulus (Eq. 1). Lateral inhibition acts as input gain control: PN = Rmax * ORN^1.5 / (ORN^1.5 + s^1.5 + sigma^1.5) (Eq. 2), where the suppression factor s rises linearly with total ORN activity (antennal LFP). Saturation reflects ORN-PN short-term depression and PN refractoriness; Rmax and sigma are similar across glomeruli — [Olsen, Bhandawat & Wilson 2010, Neuron](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2866644/). Fitted values quoted in indexed text: Rmax = 170, 167, 163, 144 and sigma = 16.3, 11.8, 12.4, 44.8 (spikes/s). The last sigma is from the glomerulus fitted without GABA antagonists; verify against Fig. 1B.
- ORN-to-PN synapses are strong and reliable: unitary EPSC 29.0 +/- 2.6 pA (n = 45) and unitary EPSP 6.19 +/- 0.45 mV (n = 23). EPSPs are similar across glomeruli despite differing EPSCs (homeostatic matching). DM4 ORN spontaneous rate is about 4 spikes/s — [Kazama & Wilson 2008, Neuron](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2429849/).
- Hemibrain: about 2,600 antennal-lobe receptor neurons (ALRNs) were identified projecting to 58 glomeruli of the right antennal lobe; 19 glomeruli are truncated and 8 more have very fragmented ALRNs, leaving 31 fully intact glomeruli (all 7 thermo/hygro plus 24 olfactory). 56 uniglomerular PN (uPN) types were compared across three hemispheres (17/56 vary in number); uPNs get about 50% of input from ALRNs vs 30% for multiglomerular PNs; hemibrain v1.1 has 188 ALPN types — [Schlegel et al. 2021, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/66018).
- MaleCNS: 71 uniglomerular ALPN types (284 neurons), 117 multiglomerular types (409 neurons) and 6 ALON types (14 neurons). "RNs" covers olfactory, thermosensory and hygrosensory receptor neurons. Sensory neurons are hard to reconstruct (fine calibre in the antennal commissure; truncation artefacts from specimen preparation). ORN counts differ between sexes for some (pheromone) glomeruli — [MaleCNS preprint](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/).
- Co-receptors are broadly co-expressed (Orco, Ir8a, Ir76b, Ir25a); Ir25a is in most olfactory neurons including most Orco+ ones; two new sacculus OSN classes target previously unidentified posterior glomerular subdivisions — [Task et al. 2022, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/72599).
- CO2-responsive antennal neurons co-express Gr21a and Gr63a, which together suffice for CO2 responses — [Jones et al. 2007, Nature (abstract)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature05466).
- NeuroMechFly v2 olfaction: virtual odour sensors on both antennae and maxillary palps read a k-dimensional odour space (observation k x 4). The diffusion function is user-defined; the example uses I(d) = I_peak/d^2, summed over sources. Antenna and palp readings are weighted 9:1 ("roughly comparable to the ratio of ORNs"). The chemotaxis controller updates every 0.05 s with gains gamma_attractive = -500 and gamma_aversive = 80 — [NeuroMechFly v2 postprint](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf).
- NeuroMechFly v2 complex plume (Supplementary Note 10): 2D incompressible Navier-Stokes with an advected scalar, in PhiFlow 2.5.3. Parameters: density 1; inflow i = 0.2 inside a 2-mm-radius source at (10, 80) mm; initial wind u = [1, 0]; random forcing as mean-reverting Brownian motion (c = 1, Sigma = 0.2I). Viscous and scalar diffusion terms were zeroed, relying on numerical diffusion. Velocity grid 1 mm, concentration grid 0.5 mm, time step 1/140 s. The encounter-driven navigation after Demir et al. 2020 uses alpha_turn = 0.8 Hz^-1, tau_turn = 2 s, turn duration 0.25 s, lambda_walk-to-stop,0 = 0.78 s^-1, delta-lambda = -0.8 s^-1, tau = 0.2 s — [NeuroMechFly v2 postprint](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf).
- Real walking-fly plume assay: main flow 150 mm/s perturbed by lateral jets (1,500 mm/s) switching stochastically on a roughly 100 ms timescale (Poisson rate 10/s); smoke imaged at 90 Hz; intermittency defined as the fraction of time above a sensory threshold in 2-s windows; odour-encounter timing biases stochastic walk/stop/turn decisions — [Demir et al. 2020, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/57524).
- 2026 perspective: connectomes alone do not reveal the early olfactory system's functional logic; explicit models of natural odorant environments and real-time, causal stage-by-stage processing are needed — [Lazar & Zhou 2026, arXiv (preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19290).

### Inferences
- A practical ORN drive pipeline: (1) sample plume concentration c(t) at each antenna and palp (NeuroMechFly-style sensors); (2) for each receptor type, map c to receptor occupancy with a Hill function whose efficacy comes from DoOR's normalised response (EC50s must be taken from dose-response studies or assumed, since DoOR has no concentration axis); (3) pass it through a Nagel-Wilson two-stage filter (transduction low-pass with adaptive gain, then a biphasic differentiating spike filter); (4) generate Poisson spikes for every connectome neuron of that ORN type, respecting ipsi/contra laterality; (5) let the connectome build PN responses. Use Olsen's Eq. 2 as a validation target for the emergent PN input-output function, not as an imposed transform.
- Calibration check for a Shiu-style LIF (w_syn = 0.275 mV per synapse): the summed synapse count of a single ORN onto a uPN times w_syn should come out near the measured 6 mV unitary EPSP. With 0.275 mV per synapse this implies roughly 20+ synapses per ORN-uPN pair; the actual per-pair counts in MaleCNS were not checked here.
- The 9:1 antenna:palp weighting in NeuroMechFly is a crude population proxy. In a connectome-driven model each ORN type (antennal vs palp) should get its own drive, with its own receptor tuning.
- NeuroMechFly's 2D plume omits molecular diffusion and 3D structure. Validate intermittency and whiff-duration statistics against Demir et al. before trusting encounter-based behaviour.

### Gaps
- Per-glomerulus ORN counts, per-antenna totals (about 1,300 per antenna is implied by about 2,600 bilateral ALRNs in one hemibrain antennal lobe, but a direct count was not extracted) and palp counts were not verified.
- A verified receptor-to-glomerulus-to-MaleCNS-type lookup table was not assembled; DoOR's mapping file plus neuPrint type names ("ORN_DA1" style) would be the starting point.
- Hallem & Carlson's exact receptor count (commonly cited as 24 receptors x 110 odorants) was not confirmed from the abstract.
- Mixture interactions (antagonism at the receptor), concentration-dependent recruitment, and ORN-type-specific kinetics (Martelli 2013; Lazar's molecular transduction model) were not extracted.
- 3D plume datasets usable at fly scale (other than NeuroMechFly's) were not surveyed.

## 3. Hearing and Johnston's organ: JON classes, antenna mechanics, and courtship-song stimuli

### Takeaway
About 480-500 Johnston's organ neurons (JONs) in antennal segment a2 are driven by rotation of the a3/arista receiver. Classes A and B (about 200 neurons) encode sound-induced vibration, with B preferring low frequencies (<~100 Hz) and A higher ones. C and E (about 250) encode maintained deflection (gravity, wind). D is less characterised, and EM work added zone-F JONs (formerly aJO) and multi-zone JONs. The receiver is an actively amplified, moderately damped oscillator: best frequency about 184 Hz in living flies vs about 800 Hz when amplification is lost. Auditory JONs respond to about 50 nm vibrations, while C/E need >200 nm to micrometre deflections. Song stimuli: pulse carrier about 220 Hz (two pulse modes: Pslow 200-250 Hz, Pfast 250-400 Hz), sine song 120-180 Hz, inter-pulse interval about 35 ms.

### Cited Findings
- JONs fall into five anatomical subgroups (A-E) targeting distinct AMMC zones. A and B are vibration-sensitive sound sensors (B prefers <~100 Hz, A higher frequencies); C and E respond maximally to static deflection and are the major gravity and wind sensors — [Kamikouchi et al. 2009, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07810) (as summarised in indexed text); see also [Matsuo et al. 2014, Front Physiol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4023023/) for newly identified vibration- and deflection-sensitive subgroups.
- The fly's roughly 500 JONs: about 200 in classes A+B (sound; required for hearing) and about 250 in C+E (maintained deflection; gravity/wind). Vibrations of 50 nm suffice to excite A/B; vibrations >200 nm also activate C/E; proprioceptive roles need several-micrometre deflections. Active amplification requires A/B but not C/E JONs. MET gating adds a nonlinear compliance consistent with a gating-spring model, and a transduction-based model captures the intensity dependence of amplification. JON motility actively tunes the antenna to courtship-song frequencies. NOMPC is the candidate transducer for auditory JONs, with an unknown channel for C/E — [Albert & Gopfert 2015, Curr Opin Neurobiol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4582067/).
- Each JO subgroup is thought to contain about 100 cells; 45 JO-A and 45 JO-B axons (about half) were traced in FAFB — [Kim et al. 2020, J Comp Neurol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7676477/).
- aJO neurons were renamed JO-F (zone F). 147 JONs were reconstructed in the JO-C/E/F region of FAFB: 104 complete with synapses, 43 autosegmented — [Hampel et al. 2020, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/59976).
- In the Shiu LIF model, activating the 147 JONs (JO-C, -E, -F, -mz) recruited aBN1, aBN2, aDN1 and aDN2. JO-CE (103 synapses onto aBN1) drove aBN1 robustly but JO-F (78 synapses) did not, because of three inhibitory interneurons; confirmed by optogenetics plus calcium imaging — [Shiu et al. 2024, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/).
- Receiver best frequency was 184 +/- 18 Hz in control flies (167 +/- 31 Hz in prestin mutants). Loss of amplification gives about 800 Hz, as in "passive" tilB antennae. The stimulus was 5.3 mm/s particle velocity measured at the antenna with a particle-velocity microphone — [Kavlie et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4282873/).
- Motile JONs show power gain — [Gopfert et al. 2005, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.0405741102). Self-sustained antennal oscillations are well described by a generalised van der Pol oscillator — [Stoop et al. 2006, Eur Biophys J](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00249-006-0059-5).
- MaleCNS: low-frequency courtship song is detected by JO-B neurons, which target aPN1 (fru+, isomorphic, essential for pulse-song detection). JO neurons have segmentation issues in the dataset, and no JON sexual dimorphism was found — [MaleCNS preprint](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/).
- Song statistics (FlySongSegmenter, 5 wild-type strains): most pulses peak at about 220 Hz and about 17% peak elsewhere; the IPI distribution (>100,000 IPIs) is right-skewed and best fit by a two-Gaussian mixture; sine carrier frequency rises 10-20 Hz over a recording; no robust about 55-s IPI rhythm — [Arthur et al. 2013, BMC Biol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3599446/); [Stern 2014, BMC Biol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4071150/).
- Three song modes: sine (continuum of carriers 120-180 Hz), Pslow (symmetrical pulses, 200-250 Hz) and Pfast (biphasic/asymmetric pulses, 250-400 Hz). Mode choice contributes to amplitude modulation with distance to the female. Dataset: 99,181 ms of sine, 56,386 Pslow and 47,241 Pfast pulses from 42 NM91 males — [Clemens et al. 2018, Curr Biol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6830513/).
- D. melanogaster IPI is about 35 ms and sine song about 150 Hz; female receptivity neurons are tuned to the about 35 ms IPI — [Sci Rep 2022, "Evolutionary conservation and diversification of auditory neural circuits..."](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-27349-7) (from indexed summary).

### Inferences
- Antenna model for a physics body: treat a3 plus arista as a rotational, moderately damped oscillator driven by air particle velocity (the JO is a near-field particle-velocity sensor), with an active van der Pol-like term that shifts best frequency from about 800 Hz (passive) toward about 180-200 Hz at low intensities. Compute A/B drive from the band-passed rotation velocity/displacement (B: low-pass/low-frequency, A: higher band) and C/E drive from low-frequency deflection (gravity vector, wind). NeuroMechFly v2 already splits the antenna into three segments with rotational DoFs, so a2-a3 joint angle could supply the static deflection.
- Song generator: pulse trains (1-3 cycle pulses, 200-400 Hz carriers, IPI about 35 ms with a skewed distribution) plus sine bouts (120-180 Hz), at particle velocities of a few mm/s at the antenna (the calibration stimulus was 5.3 mm/s).
- Spike rates for JONs are not well constrained. A defensible LIF drive is rectified, band-passed receiver motion scaled to Poisson rates, validated against AMMC-B1/aPN1 and giant-fibre responses rather than JON spikes.

### Gaps
- Per-subgroup JON firing rates, phase-locking limits and adaptation time constants were not extracted.
- Whether JO-C and JO-E are selective for opposite deflection directions (push vs pull) was not verified.
- Natural song amplitude (particle velocity) at the female's arista during courtship was not verified; playback studies use widely different levels.
- JON counts per subgroup in MaleCNS/BANC were not extracted.

## 4. Taste: gustatory receptor neuron classes, locations, response data and connectome identification

### Takeaway
Labellar gustatory receptor neurons (GRNs) fall into five molecular classes: sweet (Gr64f), bitter (Gr66a), water (Ppk28), high-salt (Ppk23-glutamatergic) and low-salt (Ir94e). Together they account for more than 90% of the taste neurons mapped in the proboscis (per the Jaeger et al. digest). Each labellar palp has 31 taste sensilla (S- and L-type with 4 GRNs, I-type with 2), about 90-104 GRNs per labellum. Further GRNs sit in the pharynx, the legs (tarsi) and the anterior wing margin. Quantitative response data are mostly tip recordings (for example 16 bitter compounds x all 31 sensilla, maximum about 61 spikes/s) and calcium imaging (salt). Labellar GRNs are identified in FlyWire (FAFB), and the complete male gustatory system (brain, neck, VNC) is mapped in the MaleCNS companion paper (Cell 2026). LIF models drive GRN sets with 50-200 Hz Poisson input.

### Cited Findings
- The labellum has three sensillum types: S- and L-type with four GRNs, I-type with two. S-type: one Ppk28 (water), one Gr64f (sweet), one Gr66a (bitter, some Ppk23-chat) and one Ppk23-glut GRN. I-type: one Gr64f and one Gr66a. L-type: Ppk28, Gr64f, Ppk23-glut and an Ir94e GRN. All five classes respond to salt in some way; the eLife digest describes the five as covering "more than 90% of all the taste neurons present in flies" in the proboscis mapping. Sodium <100 mM is generally attractive; the "L2" high-salt cell responds above about 250 mM. Bitter and Ppk23-glut GRNs are two distinct high-salt pathways; Ppk23-glut salt responses need IR76b, and salt deprivation suppresses their behavioural impact. Ir94e GRNs are weakly activated by 50 mM NaCl, not by higher concentrations, and are sodium-selective at 100 mM — [Jaeger et al. 2018, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/37167).
- Each labellar palp has 31 taste hairs, most with one bitter-sensitive neuron. All 31 were tested against 16 bitter compounds (1 or 10 mM, or 1%). Most S-type sensilla were broadly tuned (9-15 of 16 compounds at >10 spikes/s) and I-types narrower (3-7 compounds); the strongest response was 10 mM caffeine in S5 (60.8 +/- 3.3 spikes/s, n = 34); five functional sensillum classes emerged — [Weiss et al. 2011, Neuron](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3033050/). Extended bitter receptor logic: [Dweck & Carlson 2020, Curr Biol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6946858/) (not read in detail).
- FAFB reconstruction: 87 labellar GRN axons in the right hemisphere and 57 in the left, out of an expected 90-104 GRNs per labellum (83-96% and 54-63% recovery). They form six morphology/connectivity groups of 7-23 GRNs occupying discrete SEZ zones — [Engert et al. 2022, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/78110).
- FlyWire taste-circuit analysis: second-order neurons are largely modality-specific, with strong sugar/water overlap (28 neurons) and weaker bitter/Ir94e (8) and bitter/water (6) overlap. Left-side labellar GRNs were used because they are better annotated — [Sci Rep 2025, "Connectomic analysis of taste circuits in Drosophila"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11821855/).
- LIF modelling (FlyWire): sugar-GRN activation at 100 Hz drove MN9 (proboscis extension) in 100% of simulations vs 1/100 with shuffled connectivity. Silencing screens used sugar-GRN rates of 50-120 Hz in 10 Hz steps. At rates giving 40 Hz MN9 firing, sugar activates 377 neurons and water 391 (250 shared). Ir94e activation inhibits MN9 (confirmed optogenetically: blocks PER to 50 mM but not 1 M sucrose). Four GRN categories were modelled: sugar, water, bitter, Ir94e — [Shiu et al. 2024, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/).
- MaleCNS: GRNs fall into six anatomically distinct subclasses based on location on the mouthparts, legs and wings (after Stocker 1994). Foreleg GRNs have dimorphic axon projections. Local leg bristle GRNs send 11% of outputs to dimorphic circuits (wing about 6%; ascending-leg, pharyngeal and labellar about 1%) — [MaleCNS preprint](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/).
- Complete male gustatory connectome: GRNs from all peripheral organs (pharynx, legs, anterior wing margin, mouthparts) reconstructed in a contiguous brain-neck-VNC volume, cross-matched across sexes, plus all feeding motor neurons — [Tastekin et al. 2025, bioRxiv (preprint)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.25.671814v2), published as ["The complete gustatory connectome of adult Drosophila..." Cell 2026](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00943-8); companion swallowing-circuit preprint [Tastekin et al. 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.25.671815v1). Organ-by-organ GRN counts were not accessible.
- Internal (pharyngeal and gut) sensory axons were systematically identified in FAFB. The hypocerebral ganglion contains enteric sensory neurons detecting sugars, sodium salts and GI distension; pharyngeal mechanosensory neurons contribute to swallowing control — [Giakoumas et al. 2026, iScience](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13545389/).

### Inferences
- In a physics body, taste needs contact events between labellum, tarsi or wing margin and substrate patches carrying chemical identity and concentration. Map each (organ, class) pair to Poisson rates with a saturating dose-response. Tip recordings put strong responses at about 10-60 spikes/s in the first 0.5 s, whereas Shiu-style optogenetic-equivalent drives use 50-200 Hz. Pick a rate scale deliberately and validate against the PER/MN9 behaviours that Shiu et al. used.
- Leg and wing GRNs enter the VNC. They can be driven directly in MaleCNS (which includes the VNC) but are absent from FlyWire/FAFB.

### Gaps
- Organ-level GRN counts (pharyngeal LSO/VCSO/DCSO, taste pegs, each leg, wing margin) and the class composition of non-labellar GRNs were not extracted; the Cell 2026 gustatory connectome is the place to get them.
- Sugar, water and low-salt dose-response parameters (EC50, maximum rate) and GRN adaptation time constants were not extracted.
- MaleCNS naming/typing of GRNs was not checked in neuPrint.

## 5. Mechanosensation: bristles, touch and grooming inputs, and other chordotonal organs

### Takeaway
The adult leg has four basic kinds of peripheral mechanoreceptor: bristles, campaniform sensilla, chordotonal organs and hair plates. Each bristle has one cholinergic mechanosensory neuron (BMN) that fires to deflections under 10 um; a single BMN spike gives reliable EPSPs in several parallel central cell classes. The head has more than 1,000 bristles, and 705 head BMNs (plus JONs) have been mapped in FAFB as somatotopic grooming inputs; JO-CE vs JO-F grooming predictions were the mechanosensory validation case for whole-brain LIF. Other chordotonal organs include the JO (Section 3), the femoral chordotonal organ (Section 6), a tegula chordotonal organ (about 14 neurons) and haltere/wing afferents.

### Cited Findings
- Adult Drosophila possess four basic types of peripheral mechanoreceptors. Bristle neurons are cholinergic (responses blocked by 1 uM MLA). A very small bristle movement (<10 um) evokes single spikes, and one bristle spike produces a reliable EPSP in every connected central cell class; a single touch axon diverges onto three central cell classes. Optogenetic stimulation of about 200-um leg regions covered 20-80 bristles; chordotonal input inhibits touch responses through GABAergic interneurons — [Tuthill & Wilson 2016, Cell](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4879191/).
- There are "over 1000 bristles located on the head". 705 BMNs were identified among EM-reconstructed neurons (555 BM-InOm neurons plus 150 of other types), organised somatotopically into parallel pathways that drive aimed head grooming — [Eichler et al. 2024, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/87602).
- JO-C, -E and -F neurons elicit antennal grooming (147 reconstructed); LIF simulations and optogenetics agree that JO-CE, but not JO-F, drives aBN1 — [Hampel et al. 2020, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/59976); [Shiu et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/).
- Wing: all 490 afferents in the left wing nerve (ADMN) were reconstructed in FANC and mapped to peripheral structures; there are about 36 proximal wing campaniform sensilla (38 matching axons); a tegula chordotonal organ has about 14 neurons in two bundles. MANC names are cross-referenced (for example SApp04, SApp10, SApp11 for small proximal CS), but MANC wing afferents are "proofread but few are annotated by peripheral identity". Only left afferents were analysed because the right side is damaged — [eLife 2026, "Peripheral anatomy and central connectivity of proprioceptive sensory neurons in the Drosophila wing"](https://elifesciences.org/articles/107867).
- NeuroMechFly v2 exposes ground-reaction forces on tibia and all five tarsal segments of each leg, plus joint angles, velocities and forces for 42 leg DoFs — [NeuroMechFly v2 postprint (Supplementary Note 3)](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf). FlyGym 2.0.2 changed the contact API (`get_ground_contact_info` now returns raw MuJoCo contact data) — [FlyGym releases](https://github.com/NeLy-EPFL/flygym/releases).

### Inferences
- Per-bristle contact is below MuJoCo's collision resolution. Approximate by computing contact force or contact onset per body segment, distributing it over the BMN populations annotated for that segment in MaleCNS/FAFB, and using a rapidly adapting (velocity- or onset-sensitive) transduction.
- For grooming validation, "dust" can be an occupancy map over head/body regions that drives the corresponding BMN or JON populations. Eichler et al.'s somatotopic maps give the region-to-type assignment.

### Gaps
- BMN adaptation kinetics, firing-rate versus deflection curves, and leg bristle counts per segment were not extracted.
- Mapping head BMN types from FAFB to MaleCNS names was not verified.

## 6. Proprioception: femoral chordotonal organ, campaniform sensilla, hair plates and halteres, and driving them from a physics body

### Takeaway
The femoral chordotonal organ (FeCO, about 135 neurons) has three functional classes. Claw neurons (about 20 labelled) encode tibia position tonically, split into flexion- and extension-tuned groups with hysteresis. Hook neurons (about 3 labelled per line) encode directional movement phasically. Club neurons (about 30) encode bidirectional movement and 100-2,000 Hz vibration in a tonotopic map. These selectivities arise from separate tendons and the arculum (Mamiya 2023). Hook, but not claw or club, afferents are presynaptically inhibited during self-generated walking and grooming via GABAergic 9A interneurons (Dallmann 2025). Hair plates (214 neurons in 42 plates across six legs) act as joint-limit detectors. Campaniform sensilla sense cuticular strain (femoral field of 11; about 36 proximal wing CS; about 400 haltere afferents in total). Calcium-level encoding models driven by femur-tibia angle already exist (Dallmann 2025) and can be fed from NeuroMechFly or flybody joint states.

### Cited Findings
- FeCO subclasses by driver line: claw about 20 cell bodies (blade-shaped strip along the femur), club about 30 (proximal FeCO) and hook 3 (ventral edge). iav-Gal4 labelled "~80% of the total population (~135 neurons)"; the phrasing is ambiguous as to whether 135 is the total or the labelled subset. Claw neurons respond tonically, with flexion-tuned activity rising between about 90 and 18 deg and extension-tuned activity between about 90 and 180 deg, neither active near 90 deg, roughly linear in angle with directional hysteresis (ramp-and-hold, 18-deg steps). Club neurons are phasic and bidirectional (DSI 0.117 +/- 0.022). Hook neurons are phasic and flexion-selective (DSI 0.811 +/- 0.028). Only club neurons reliably respond to tibia vibration (100-2,000 Hz, 0.9 or 0.054 um peak-to-peak), with a frequency map along the club axon terminals; club sensitivity (200-2,000 Hz) matches substrate vibrations during courtship — [Mamiya, Gurung & Tuthill 2018, Neuron](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6481666/).
- FeCO neurons sit in three compartments (club = largest dorsal Group 1; claw = linear array, Group 2; hook-flexion = lateral Group 3; hook-extension distal to claw in Group 2). The claw/hook tendons fuse about 100 um distal to the FeCO while the club tendon stays separate; both converge on the arculum, which couples to the tibia extensor tendon and joint. Club and hook-flexion somata move <2 um across the full joint range, whereas claw and hook-extension cells move distally during flexion. A finite-element arculum model explains the vibration-versus-position decomposition. Transcriptomic markers: club AstaA-R1, claw Dop2R, hook Ca-alpha1T — [Mamiya et al. 2023, Neuron](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10644877/).
- In behaving flies, hook (movement) axons are suppressed during walking and grooming, but claw (position) and club (vibration) axons are not. A GABAergic 9A interneuron class supplies most presynaptic inhibition to hook axons and receives descending input; all claw/hook neurons express Rdl; claw and hook axons were reconstructed in FANC. Their encoding models convolve an activation function with a GCaMP kernel exp(-t/tau_off) - exp(-t/tau_on) (tau_on = 0.03 s, tau_off = 0.3 s). Claw: a fourth-order polynomial of (femur-tibia angle - 90 deg), or -80 deg in their dataset, with weaker response on one side. Hook: a binary step on angular velocity, threshold 5 deg/s (passive dataset) or 50 deg/s (behaving); hook-extension neurons mirror hook-flexion. Club: a rectangular function of velocity (+/-5 or +/-50 deg/s) — [Dallmann et al. 2025, Nature, "Selective presynaptic inhibition of leg proprioception in behaving Drosophila"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13070307/) (2023 bioRxiv preprint).
- Hair plates: the six legs carry 214 hair-plate neurons in 42 plates. The coxal plate CxHP8 (8 neurons, all reconstructed in FANC) encodes the anterior limit of thorax-coxa movement and connects to premotor and motor neurons of the coxa-posterior motor module. Activation elicits posterior postural reflexes, and silencing alters the swing-to-stance transition. Other plates' reflexes are predicted from their position and connectivity — [Pratt et al. 2026, Nat Commun](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13009157/) (2025 bioRxiv preprint).
- Campaniform sensilla nomenclature: fields (>=3 CS in a row or column, <=10 um apart), groups (2-4 CS within 25 um) and singles, named by segment (Tr, Fe, Ti, Ta1-5) and side — [Dinges et al. 2021, J Comp Neurol](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cne.24987) (from indexed summary). The femoral CS field contains 11 CS in most flies. A parametric finite-element model (12 general plus 7 CS-specific parameters) maps kinematics and computed ground-reaction forces to strain at each CS during forward stepping — [Dinges et al. 2025, J R Soc Interface](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12056673/). Newer preprint on leg CS feedback in adaptive walking: [doi:10.64898/2026.07.22.740025](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.22.740025) (preprint; title only).
- Halteres: Drosophila halteres carry four stereotyped CS fields (blowflies have a fifth). Earlier counts suggested about 280 haltere afferents, but FANC, MANC and BANC reconstructions agree on about 400 haltere sensory afferents across both sides, perhaps because haltere chordotonal neurons were included. On the left side of FANC, 4,479 neurons receive 69,199 synapses from haltere afferents (about 40% of their output). Haltere afferents target wing-steering motor neurons in putative modules (for example CL1 to b3/i1/i2, CL2 to iii3). FANC lacks the full peripheral nerves, so axon subtypes are not yet mapped to CS fields — [Dhawan et al. 2026, Curr Biol, "A neural connectivity atlas for fly flight control"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12872070/). A 2026 behavioural study addresses haltere CS roles in equilibrium reflexes — [J Exp Biol 2026](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/229/6/jeb250431/371120/The-role-of-haltere-campaniform-sensilla-in) (not read).
- Four wing-steering motor neurons get more than 10% of their synaptic input from wing and haltere sensory neurons (citing Lesser et al. 2024); b1 receives clustered wing and haltere afferent input near its putative spike-initiation zone — [eLife 2026 wing paper](https://elifesciences.org/articles/107867).
- NeuroMechFly v2 already uses "ascending" proprioceptive and tactile signals (joint angles, binarised tarsal contact forces) for path integration and head stabilisation; the observation space includes angle, velocity and force for 42 leg DoFs and 3D contact forces per tibia and tarsal segment — [NeuroMechFly v2 postprint](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/ramdya-lab/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NMF2_postprint.pdf).

### Inferences
- A direct recipe for driving FeCO types in the connectome from a physics body:
  - Claw: rate = g(theta_FTi - 80 deg), using Dallmann's polynomial shape, split into flexion- and extension-tuned subpopulations; add hysteresis by conditioning on recent movement direction.
  - Hook: phasic bursts when d(theta)/dt exceeds about 50 deg/s in the preferred direction (flexion or extension), decaying over tens of ms. Keep the connectome's 9A presynaptic inhibition intact, or explicitly gate hook drive during self-generated movement, which Dallmann shows is biological.
  - Club: |d(theta)/dt| above threshold plus a 100-2,000 Hz vibration band-pass from substrate acceleration (the physics step must be short enough to represent vibration; otherwise inject vibration analytically).
  - The calcium-to-spike conversion is unknown, so pick peak rates (for example 50-200 Hz) and validate with downstream-neuron or behavioural data.
- Campaniform sensilla: compute per-segment cuticular load from joint torques and ground-reaction forces (NeuroMechFly/flybody). Map it to per-field strain with the Dinges finite-element model or a linear surrogate fitted to it, with phasic-plus-tonic encoding per field.
- Hair plates: threshold-linear functions of joint angle near each joint's limit (for example thorax-coxa anterior limit for CxHP8).
- Halteres need flight simulation with haltere kinematics at wingbeat frequency. Wing and haltere CS afferents then feed steering motor neurons almost monosynaptically. Putative electrical synapses have been proposed for some of these pathways (w-ChiN onto b1; CL2 afferents onto iii3), which a chemical-synapse-only LIF will not capture ([eLife 2026 wing paper](https://elifesciences.org/articles/107867); [Dhawan et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12872070/)).

### Gaps
- FeCO spike-rate (not calcium) tuning curves, and the transfer from joint angle to rate at walking speeds, were not found.
- Total CS per leg and per segment (Dinges 2021 tables), CS strain thresholds and adaptation kinetics were not extracted.
- Haltere and wing CS firing phase relative to the wingbeat was not extracted for Drosophila.
- Mapping claw/hook/club, hair-plate and CS axon types to MaleCNS/MANC type names (for example "SNpp", "SApp") needs cross-referencing with FANC annotations.

## 7. Thermo-, hygro-, CO2 and internal-state sensing (brief)

### Takeaway
Each arista holds six thermosensory neurons, three hot and three cold, that respond to changes of about 0.5 C and track stimulus kinetics. The sacculus adds cold-responsive neurons and hygrosensors: dry air via Ir40a to glomerulus VP4, humid air via Ir68a to VP5. Heating cells project to VP2 (Gr28b.d) and cooling cells to VP3 (Ir21a); VP1d/l/m receive further saccular classes. CO2 is sensed by Gr21a/Gr63a antennal neurons. Internal-state sensors include Gr43a brain neurons that detect hemolymph fructose, enteric neurons in the hypocerebral ganglion (sugar, sodium, distension) and aorta sensory neurons. All of these are identifiable in FAFB, hemibrain and MaleCNS, and several have recently been mapped in BANC.

### Cited Findings
- The arista has six neurons: three activated by warming and three by cooling, each responding to changes as small as about 0.5 C and closely tracking the stimulus. Brivido (brv1) marks the cold cells, and about 15-20 brv1-expressing neurons in the sacculus also respond to cooling. Wild-type flies prefer 24-27 C — [Gallio et al. 2011, Cell](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3336488/).
- Glomerular targets:
  - VP2 (heating): aristal Gr28b.d neurons; 3 right-hand and 4 left-hand receptor neurons in FAFB.
  - VP3 (cooling): Ir21a neurons; 4 right and 5 left, including one "simpler" cell that feeds the slow-cool VP3 PN.
  - VP4 (dry air): Ir40a; 28 bilateral receptor neurons.
  - VP5 (humid air): Ir68a.
  - VP1d/l/m: other saccular classes (Ir40a chamber II cells reported to respond weakly to cooling, dry air or ammonia; VP1l Ir21a+; VP1m Ir68a+).
  - Downstream, a slow-adapting cool-sensing VP3 PN targets the lateral accessory calyx of the mushroom body, and fast-adapting thermosensory PNs target the posterior lateral protocerebrum — [Marin et al. 2020, Curr Biol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7443704/).
- MaleCNS flow analysis: VP4 dry-air projection neurons are the strongest upstream contributors to DNp44, a route from olfactory/hygrosensory input to front-leg motor output — [MaleCNS preprint](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/).
- CO2: Gr21a and Gr63a are co-expressed in CO2-responsive antennal neurons and together suffice for CO2 detection — [Jones et al. 2007, Nature (abstract)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature05466).
- Internal nutrient sensing: Gr43a is a narrowly tuned fructose receptor in taste neurons and in brain neurons. Hemolymph fructose rises several-fold after carbohydrate intake, enough to activate brain Gr43a neurons, which promote feeding when hungry and suppress it when satiated — [Miyamoto et al. 2012, Cell (abstract)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3509419/).
- Enteric sensory neurons in the hypocerebral ganglion detect sugars, sodium salts and gut distension; pharyngeal and gut sensory axons are now mapped in FAFB — [Giakoumas et al. 2026, iScience](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13545389/). BANC: ascending neurons (e.g. AN27X017) project directly onto insulin-producing endocrine cells and converge there with aorta sensory neurons — [Bates et al. 2026, Nature (BANC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13518251/).

### Inferences
- Thermo- and hygrosensors respond to change, so drive them with phasic (derivative) plus tonic components of the temperature and humidity at the antenna. Aristal cells respond within the resolution of calcium imaging to 0.5 C steps. A simple model: rate = a * max(0, dT/dt) for hot cells (mirrored for cold), plus a small tonic term.
- Internal-state inputs (hemolymph sugar, gut distension) are slow and could enter a closed-loop simulation as slowly varying biases on the relevant sensory or neuroendocrine neurons.

### Gaps
- Firing-rate models and time constants for thermo/hygro receptor neurons (for example Frank et al. 2015; Budelli et al. 2019; Knecht et al. 2017) were not extracted.
- CO2 neuron counts (ab1C, V glomerulus) and dose-response were not extracted.
- Internal-sensor identities in MaleCNS were not checked.

## 8. Connectome coverage: which sensory populations are identified in MaleCNS, FlyWire/FAFB, BANC, MANC and FANC, and where they are truncated or missing

### Takeaway
All EM connectomes contain only the central axon terminals of sensory neurons. Somata, dendrites and transduction sites lie in the periphery, so identity comes from the entry nerve, axon morphology and matching to genetic lines.
- MaleCNS v1.0 (Cell, 3 September 2026; 166,691 to about 166,700 neurons, 25.6M edges) covers brain, optic lobes and VNC, with sensory neurons annotated by modality and receptor subtype. Its laminae are only partially reconstructed (many R1-6 and Lai missing), the ocellar ganglion is reported missing, and JONs and ORNs have segmentation problems.
- FlyWire/FAFB (female brain only, 139,255 neurons) includes lamina and ocellar ganglion, but photoreceptors are under-recovered and VNC-borne leg, wing and haltere afferents are absent.
- BANC (female brain plus VNC, Nature 2026) lacks the laminae and ocellar ganglion (about 9,390 R1-6/Lai neurons missing).
- MANC and FANC cover the VNC only, with leg, wing and haltere afferents; FANC's right wing nerve is damaged.
- The hemibrain truncates about half of the antennal-lobe glomeruli.

### Cited Findings
- MaleCNS (preprint, bioRxiv Oct 2025): 166,691 neurons "including sensory axons" across the entire volume, 11,691 types; 25.6M edges between 166,391 neurons; sensory and motor neurons comprehensively mapped with receptor subtype for sensory neurons and exit nerve/muscle for motor neurons. Some cells were unrecoverable "due to sample artefacts on the edge of the volume (e.g. some R1-6 photoreceptor neurons in the laminae) or segmentation issues (some sensory and motor neurons)". About 900 optic-lobe columns (FAFB about 800). The superclass scheme includes "sensory ascending" and "sensory descending" neurons — [MaleCNS preprint (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.09.680999v1). Published in Cell on 3 September 2026 with 166,700 neurons and 11,710 types — [Berg et al. 2026, Cell](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00942-6) (count from indexed summary); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42691995). Served via neuPrint as MaleCNS v1.0 — [Fly Connectome ecosystem post, Sept 2026](https://flyconnecto.me/2026/09/04/the-adult-drosophila-connectome-ecosystem/).
- The male right optic lobe connectome (the optic-lobe subset of the same animal) has about 53,000 neurons, 732 types (published) and about 49M synaptic connections (connection count from indexed summary). Its lamina is incomplete (stair-step edges, about 47% connection completeness); R1-6, Lai and some R7/R8 are undercounted — [Nern et al. 2025, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08746-0); [PMC preprint version (727 types)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11042306/). Type counts differ between preprint (727) and published (732) versions.
- BANC: "As in the independent maleCNS project, both the lamina and the ocellar ganglion are missing; however, these regions are present in the FAFB dataset." R1-R6 and Lai (about 9,390 neurons) are absent because the laminae are outside the volume. 155,916 proofread or roughly proofread neurons, 147,846 linked to cell-type labels from other datasets or the literature, and 13,108 glia/trachea among identified cells. Every axon in nerves leaving the dataset and at both neck levels was verified as attached to a segmented neuron — [Bates et al. 2026, Nature (BANC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13518251/) (bioRxiv 2025 preprint). Contradiction: BANC says the MaleCNS lamina is "missing", while the MaleCNS and Nern papers describe a partial, incomplete lamina with some R1-6 present. The user's MaleCNS model does contain R1-6-to-lamina synapses, which fits "partial".
- FlyWire/FAFB: 139,255 neurons and 54.5M synapses. Neuropil volumes include the lamina (optic lobes 0.0036 mm^3 each). 25 ocellar centrifugal neurons feed back to the ocellar ganglion — [Dorkenwald et al. 2024, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446842/). Composition: 32,388 central-brain intrinsic, 77,536 optic-lobe intrinsic, 8,053 visual projection and 524 visual centrifugal neurons; afferent input via 5,512 sensory and 2,362 ascending neurons; output via 1,303 descending, 80 endocrine and 106 motor neurons. Head mechanosensory bristle, taste-peg, gustatory and JO neurons were identified from prior work — [Schlegel et al. 2024, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446831/). Photoreceptors: R7 and R8 about 650 each and R1-6 about 3,400 in v783, under-recovered because they are hard to proofread — [Matsliah et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446827/).
- Hemibrain: about 2,600 ALRNs to 58 glomeruli; 19 glomeruli truncated and 8 more fragmented, so only 31 glomeruli are fully usable — [Schlegel et al. 2021, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/66018). The MaleCNS paper notes about 50% of hemibrain glomeruli are truncated or fragmented — [MaleCNS preprint](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12636603/).
- MANC (male VNC): more than 23,000 neurons, >10M presynapses and 74M postsynapses. Reconstructed neurons include sensory neurons (somata in the periphery), descending, motor, intrinsic, ascending and efferent neurons, with systematic annotation of sensory neurons by inferred modality/type — [Takemura et al. 2024, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/97769); [Marin et al. 2024, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/97766); [Cheong et al., eLife](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13384506/). Wing sensory axons are proofread but few are annotated by peripheral identity; the 2026 wing paper gives a FANC-to-MANC name table — [eLife 2026](https://elifesciences.org/articles/107867).
- FANC (female VNC): used for complete reconstructions of the left wing nerve (490 afferents), claw/hook axons, CxHP8 hair-plate axons and haltere afferents (about 400 across both sides, corroborated in MANC and BANC). FANC "does not include the full peripheral nerves", and the right wing nerve is damaged — [eLife 2026 wing paper](https://elifesciences.org/articles/107867); [Dhawan et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12872070/); [Dallmann et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13070307/); [Pratt et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13009157/); dataset papers [Azevedo et al. 2024, Nature](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11348827/) and [Phelps et al. 2021, Cell](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8312698/).
- Gustatory coverage: FAFB labellar GRNs 87 right and 57 left (54-96% of expected), limited by misalignments — [Engert et al. 2022](https://elifesciences.org/articles/78110). The complete male gustatory system is in MaleCNS — [Tastekin et al., Cell 2026](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00943-8).
- An ecosystem summary (Sept 2026) lists BANC at about 142,000 neurons and MANC at about 15,800, which conflicts with the primary papers (BANC 155,916 proofread or roughly proofread; MANC >23,000 including sensory fragments). The definitions probably differ; prefer the primary papers — [flyconnecto.me](https://flyconnecto.me/2026/09/04/the-adult-drosophila-connectome-ecosystem/).

### Inferences
- For the MaleCNS LIF model, sensory drive should target neurons whose superclass is sensory (and sensory ascending/descending), using the modality and receptor-subtype annotations.
  - Vision: expect an incomplete R1-6 set per column. Either synthesise the missing photoreceptors' input onto L1-L3 of each column (inferring column identity from medulla coordinates), or inject at L1-L3/R7/R8. There is no ocellar input.
  - Olfaction: ORN types by glomerulus are present but some fragmented. Check ORN counts per glomerulus against FAFB before assigning Poisson rates, since per-neuron rate x neuron count sets the total drive.
  - Mechanosensation and proprioception: leg, wing and haltere afferents are in the VNC part of MaleCNS, so a MaleCNS brain+VNC model can take proprioceptive and tactile drive from a physics body directly. FAFB-only models would have to approximate this through ascending neurons.
- Because every connectome truncates sensory neurons at the nerve, all transduction (receptor kinetics, adaptation, mechanics) must be modelled outside the connectome, and the connectome's sensory "neurons" should be treated as spike generators driven by those peripheral models.
- Cross-dataset validation: FAFB (lamina, ocelli present) for photoreceptor-to-lamina tests; BANC/MaleCNS for brain-VNC loops; FANC for leg/wing/haltere afferent identity; MANC for type names.

### Gaps
- Per-modality sensory neuron counts for MaleCNS v1.0 and MANC were not extracted (they are in figures and neuPrint, not the article text).
- Whether the MaleCNS ocellar ganglion is wholly absent (BANC's claim) or partially present was not verified from MaleCNS sources.
- FlyWire's per-modality sensory breakdown (Dorkenwald 2024, Fig. 2e) was not transcribed.
- Peripheral-location metadata for individual sensory axons (which bristle, which CS field) exists only for subsets (head BMNs, FeCO, hair plates, wing, haltere); organism-wide mapping remains incomplete.
